# history | British Rule and Freedom

## his-tilak

= chirol
Q Who called Tilak the "Father of Indian Unrest"?
A Valentine Chirol
N He was a British journalist. He said it in his book "Indian Unrest" (1910).
Q Valentine Chirol's book "Indian Unrest" gave a famous title to which leader?
A Bal Gangadhar Tilak
S An insult worn as a medal | In 1910 a British journalist, **Valentine Chirol**, wrote a book to explain why India was angry. He blamed one man and called him the **Father of Indian Unrest**. **Tilak** went to London and took him to court for it. He lost the case and a great deal of money. But in India the insult was read as praise. If the British thought one man had woken the country, that man must be worth following.

= tilak
Q Which slogan is Tilak remembered for?
A "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it"
Q By which title, meaning "honoured by the people", is Tilak known?
A Lokmanya
Q Which two festivals did Tilak turn into public gatherings?
A The Ganapati festival and the Shivaji festival
N Ganapati in 1893 and Shivaji in 1895. A religious crowd could meet where a political one could not.
Q In which jail was Tilak kept from 1908 to 1914, and which book did he write there?
A Mandalay (Burma); Gita Rahasya
Q Tilak died on 1 August 1920. Which movement began on that same day?
A The Non-Cooperation Movement
F Tilak in five lines | Newspapers: **Kesari** (Marathi) and **Mahratta** (English). Festivals: Ganapati and Shivaji. Slogan: "Swaraj is my birthright". Jail: **Mandalay**, 1908–14, where he wrote **Gita Rahasya**. Last work: the **Home Rule League** and the **Lucknow Pact**, both 1916.

= lalbalpal
Q Who were Lal, Bal and Pal?
A Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak and Bipin Chandra Pal
N Punjab, Maharashtra and Bengal. They led the Extremists in the Congress.
Q Bipin Chandra Pal belonged to which province?
A Bengal
N He edited New India and worked on Bande Mataram.
F Moderates and Extremists | The **Moderates**, such as Gokhale and Pherozeshah Mehta, sent petitions and trusted British fairness. The **Extremists**, **Lal, Bal and Pal**, wanted boycott, swadeshi and mass action. The two groups broke apart at **Surat in 1907** and came together again at **Lucknow in 1916**.

= surat
D 1907 | Surat split | The Congress broke in two at its Surat session. The Moderates kept the party; the Extremists under Tilak were shut out for nine years. **Rash Behari Ghosh** was the president of the session.
Q At which session did the Congress split into Moderates and Extremists, and in which year?
A Surat, 1907
Q Who presided over the Surat session of 1907?
A Rash Behari Ghosh

= bengal1905
D 1905 | Partition of Bengal | **Lord Curzon** cut Bengal in two. He said the province was too big to govern. Indians saw a plan to divide Hindus and Muslims. The answer was the **Swadeshi movement**: boycott foreign cloth, buy Indian.
Q Which Viceroy partitioned Bengal in 1905?
A Lord Curzon
Q Which movement began as the answer to the Partition of Bengal?
A The Swadeshi movement
Q In which year was the Partition of Bengal cancelled, and what else was announced with it?
A 1911; the capital would move from Calcutta to Delhi
N Lord Hardinge was the Viceroy. King George V announced it at the Delhi Durbar.
Q Where and in which year was the Muslim League founded?
A Dacca, 1906

= homerule
D 1916 | Two Home Rule Leagues | **Tilak** started his league in April, for Maharashtra and Karnataka. **Annie Besant** started hers in September from Madras, for the rest of India. Both asked for self-government inside the British Empire.
Q Who started the two Home Rule Leagues of 1916?
A Tilak and Annie Besant
N Tilak's came first, in April. Besant's followed in September.
Q What did the Lucknow Pact of 1916 bring together?
A The Congress and the Muslim League
N At the same session the Moderates and Extremists also came back together.
Q Who presided over the Lucknow session of 1916?
A Ambika Charan Mazumdar

= lajpat
Q Which leader is called "Punjab Kesari"?
A Lala Lajpat Rai
Q Which newspaper did Lala Lajpat Rai found?
A The Punjabee
Q Who was the first president of the All India Trade Union Congress?
A Lala Lajpat Rai
N The year was 1920.
Q Lala Lajpat Rai died of injuries received in a protest against what?
A The Simon Commission
N The police lathi-charged the march at Lahore in October 1928. He died on 17 November.
Q Which society did Lala Lajpat Rai found?
A Servants of the People Society
N Do not mix it up with Gokhale's Servants of India Society (1905).
S The blows at Lahore | On 30 October 1928 the Simon Commission reached Lahore station. A silent crowd stood there with black flags, led by an old man, **Lala Lajpat Rai**. The police charged with lathis and the blows fell on his chest. That evening he said that every blow on his body would prove a nail in the coffin of British rule. He died on **17 November**. A month later a young man named **Bhagat Singh** shot a police officer, Saunders, in revenge.

= gokhale
Q Who founded the Servants of India Society, and in which year?
A Gopal Krishna Gokhale, 1905
Q Whom did Gandhi call his political guru?
A Gopal Krishna Gokhale

## his-gandhi

= return
D 1915 | Gandhi comes home | Gandhi landed at Bombay from South Africa on **9 January 1915**. The day is now kept as Pravasi Bharatiya Divas. On Gokhale's advice he spent a year travelling and listening before he spoke on Indian politics.
Q On which date did Gandhi return to India from South Africa?
A 9 January 1915
N The day is now Pravasi Bharatiya Divas.

= fourfights
Q Put in order: Kheda, Champaran, Rowlatt Satyagraha, Ahmedabad Mill Strike.
A Champaran, Ahmedabad, Kheda, Rowlatt
N 1917, early 1918, 1918, 1919.
F Four small fights | Gandhi tested satyagraha four times before he used it on the whole country. **Champaran 1917**: indigo farmers. **Ahmedabad 1918**: mill workers, and his first hunger strike. **Kheda 1918**: farmers who could not pay tax. **Rowlatt 1919**: his first all-India call.

= champaran
Q Which was Gandhi's first satyagraha in India, and in which year?
A Champaran, 1917
Q The Champaran satyagraha was fought for farmers forced to grow which crop?
A Indigo
N Under the "tinkathia" system a farmer had to grow indigo on three parts in twenty of his land.
Q Champaran is in which State?
A Bihar
S The farmer who would not go away | In 1916 a farmer from Champaran named **Rajkumar Shukla** came to the Lucknow Congress. He wanted someone to see how the indigo planters treated his people. He picked Gandhi, who had never heard of Champaran. Shukla followed him to Kanpur, then to Ahmedabad, then to Calcutta, asking each time. At last Gandhi gave in. In **1917** he reached Champaran, refused an order to leave, and won. It was his **first satyagraha in India**.

= ahmedabad
Q In which struggle did Gandhi go on his first hunger strike?
A Ahmedabad Mill Strike, 1918
N The mill workers were asking for higher wages. They got a 35 per cent rise.

= kheda
Q What was the Kheda satyagraha of 1918 about?
A Farmers refused to pay land tax after their crops failed
Q Kheda is in which State, and which future leader joined Gandhi there?
A Gujarat; Vallabhbhai Patel

= rowlatt
Q What did the Rowlatt Act of 1919 allow the government to do?
A Jail people without trial
N People called it the Black Act: "no appeal, no lawyer, no argument".
D 1919 | Jallianwala Bagh | On **13 April**, the day of Baisakhi, **General Dyer** closed the only way out of a walled garden at Amritsar and ordered his men to fire on the crowd. **Tagore** gave up his knighthood in protest.
Q On which date did the Jallianwala Bagh massacre take place?
A 13 April 1919
Q Who ordered the firing at Jallianwala Bagh?
A General Dyer
Q Who gave up his knighthood after Jallianwala Bagh?
A Rabindranath Tagore
N Gandhi gave back his Kaiser-i-Hind medal.
Q Which committee inquired into the Jallianwala Bagh firing?
A Hunter Committee
N This is a different Hunter from the education commission of 1882.

= national
Q Put in order: Quit India, Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, Simon Commission boycott.
A Non-Cooperation, Simon boycott, Civil Disobedience, Quit India
N 1920, 1928, 1930, 1942.
F Three movements, three endings | **Non-Cooperation (1920–22)** ended when Gandhi called it off after Chauri Chaura. **Civil Disobedience (1930–34)** began with salt and paused with the Gandhi–Irwin Pact. **Quit India (1942)** had no pause: the leaders were jailed on the first morning and the people carried on alone.

= noncoop
Q After which event did Gandhi call off the Non-Cooperation Movement?
A Chauri Chaura, February 1922
N A crowd set fire to a police station in Gorakhpur district and 22 policemen died.
Q Which party did C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru form in 1923, after Non-Cooperation was called off?
A Swaraj Party

= simon
Q Why did Indians boycott the Simon Commission?
A It had no Indian member
N All seven members were British. It came in 1928 and was met with "Simon Go Back".

= salt
D 1930 | Dandi March | Gandhi left **Sabarmati Ashram** on **12 March** with **78 followers**. He walked for 24 days to the sea at Dandi and picked up salt on **6 April**. That one act began the Civil Disobedience Movement.
Q On which date did Gandhi break the salt law at Dandi?
A 6 April 1930
Q From which place did the Dandi March begin?
A Sabarmati Ashram
N It began on 12 March 1930 with 78 followers.
Q Which movement began with the Dandi March?
A Civil Disobedience Movement
F Odisha's Dandi | When Gandhi marched to Dandi, Odisha marched too. A party set out from Cuttack under **Gopabandhu Choudhury** and **Acharya Harihar** for **Inchudi** on the Balasore coast, and salt was made there in April 1930. Inchudi is remembered as the second great centre of the salt satyagraha after Dandi.
Q Which place in Balasore district was the centre of the salt satyagraha in Odisha?
A Inchudi

= pact
Q Which pact of March 1931 paused the Civil Disobedience Movement?
A Gandhi–Irwin Pact
Q Which Round Table Conference did Gandhi attend?
A The Second, in 1931
N He went as the only speaker for the Congress. The Congress stayed away from the first and the third.
Q What did the Poona Pact of 1932 settle, and between whom?
A Reserved seats for the Depressed Classes; Gandhi and Ambedkar
N Ambedkar gave up separate electorates in return for more reserved seats.

= sessions
Q Which was the only Congress session over which Gandhi presided?
A Belgaum, 1924
Q Who presided over the Lahore session of 1929, and what goal was adopted there?
A Jawaharlal Nehru; Purna Swaraj
Q On which date was the first Independence Day kept?
A 26 January 1930
N That is why the Constitution came into force on 26 January, twenty years later.
Q Which Congress session was the first to be held in a village?
A Faizpur, 1936
Q At which session did Subhas Bose defeat Pattabhi Sitaramayya?
A Tripuri, 1939
N Sitaramayya was Gandhi's choice. Bose won, then resigned, and formed the Forward Bloc.
Q Who was the first woman president of the Congress, and who was the first Indian woman?
A Annie Besant (1917); Sarojini Naidu (1925)
F Sessions worth one mark each | **Belgaum 1924**: Gandhi's only time as president. **Lahore 1929**: Nehru, Purna Swaraj. **Karachi 1931**: Patel, Fundamental Rights. **Faizpur 1936**: first village session. **Haripura 1938** and **Tripuri 1939**: Subhas Bose.

= karachi
D 1931 | Karachi session | It met days after **Bhagat Singh** was hanged. **Sardar Patel** presided. The session approved the Gandhi–Irwin Pact and passed the resolution on **Fundamental Rights** and the National Economic Programme.
Q Who presided over the Karachi session of 1931?
A Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel
N The 2025 paper offered Motilal Nehru. He had died in February 1931, before the session met.
Q Which famous resolution was passed at the Karachi session of 1931?
A Fundamental Rights and the National Economic Programme
Q On which date were Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev and Rajguru hanged?
A 23 March 1931

= quit
D 1942 | Quit India | On **8 August** the Congress met at Bombay and asked the British to leave at once. Gandhi gave the call **"Do or Die"**. By the next morning every leader was in jail.
Q Which slogan did Gandhi give with the Quit India resolution?
A "Do or Die"
Q In which city, and on which date, was the Quit India resolution passed?
A Bombay, 8 August 1942
Q The failure of which mission in 1942 led to the Quit India Movement?
A Cripps Mission
