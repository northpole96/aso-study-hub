# history | British Rule and Freedom

## his-viceroys

= lytton
D 1877 | Delhi Durbar | **Lord Lytton** held a grand durbar at Delhi to declare **Queen Victoria the Empress of India**. A terrible famine was killing people in the south at the same time.
Q Which Viceroy held the Delhi Durbar of 1877?
A Lord Lytton
Q What was announced at the Delhi Durbar of 1877?
A Queen Victoria was declared Empress of India
Q Which Viceroy passed the Vernacular Press Act, and in which year?
A Lord Lytton, 1878
Q Which other Act of 1878 stopped Indians from keeping weapons without a licence?
A The Arms Act
Q The Vernacular Press Act applied to newspapers in which languages?
A Indian languages only
N English papers were left alone. That is why it was called the "Gagging Act".
Q Lytton cut the top age for the civil service exam from 21 to what?
A 19
F Lytton's black list | Learn **Lord Lytton (1876–80)** as the Viceroy who made India angry. He held the **Delhi Durbar (1877)** during a famine. He passed the **Vernacular Press Act** and the **Arms Act** in 1878. He cut the civil service age to 19. Each one gave the early nationalists something to fight.
S The paper that changed its language overnight | The **Vernacular Press Act of 1878** was aimed at Indian-language papers. The sharpest of them was the **Amrita Bazar Patrika** of **Sisir Kumar Ghosh**, printed in Bengali. The Act could not touch an English paper. So the brothers who ran it did a simple thing. The very next issue came out fully in **English**. The law had nothing to hold on to, and the paper went on scolding the government for many more years.

= ripon
D 1882 | Ripon's Resolution | **Lord Ripon** asked for local boards in towns and districts, with most members elected and not chosen by officials. For this he is called the **Father of Local Self-Government** in India.
Q Which Viceroy is called the Father of Local Self-Government in India?
A Lord Ripon
N The name comes from his Resolution of 1882.
Q Who repealed the Vernacular Press Act, and in which year?
A Lord Ripon, 1882
Q In which year was the first Factory Act passed, and under which Viceroy?
A 1881, Lord Ripon
N It was meant to protect child workers.
Q What did the Ilbert Bill of 1883 try to allow?
A Indian judges to try Europeans
Q Who was Ilbert, after whom the Ilbert Bill is named?
A The Law Member of Ripon's council
N His full name was Courtenay Ilbert.
Q Which education commission did Ripon appoint in 1882?
A Hunter Commission
N It looked mainly at primary and secondary schools.
Q The first regular census of all India was taken in which year?
A 1881
N A first try was made in 1872 under Lord Mayo. From 1881 it has been held every ten years.
F What the Ilbert Bill taught India | The Europeans in India rose in anger against the **Ilbert Bill**, and **Ripon** had to weaken it. Indians watched and learnt a lesson: a noisy, organised protest can bend the government. Two years later, in 1885, the Congress was born.
F Lytton and Ripon side by side | **Lytton** passed the Vernacular Press Act (1878); **Ripon** repealed it (1882). Lytton passed the Arms Act; Ripon passed the first **Factory Act (1881)**. Lytton held a durbar; Ripon gave **local self-government (1882)**, the **Hunter Commission (1882)** and the **Ilbert Bill (1883)**.

= wood
D 1854 | Wood's Despatch | **Charles Wood** asked for a full ladder of schools, from the village school to the university, and for a department of education in every province. It is called the **Magna Carta of English Education in India**.
Q What is called the Magna Carta of English Education in India?
A Wood's Despatch, 1854
Q Which post did Charles Wood hold when he sent his Despatch?
A President of the Board of Control
Q Which three universities opened in 1857?
A Calcutta, Bombay and Madras
Q Who was the Governor-General when Wood's Despatch arrived?
A Lord Dalhousie

= macaulay
D 1835 | Macaulay's Minute | **Macaulay** argued that the government's money should go to teaching Western learning through **English**. The Governor-General, **William Bentinck**, agreed, and English became the language of higher education.
Q What did Macaulay's Minute of 1835 decide?
A English would be the language of teaching
Q Which Governor-General accepted Macaulay's Minute?
A Lord William Bentinck
F The downward filter | Macaulay did not plan to teach every Indian. He wanted to educate a small class in English and let knowledge "filter down" from them to the people. This is called the **downward filtration theory**. Wood's Despatch of 1854 gave it up and asked for mass schooling.

Say it as one line: **Hunter 1882, Raleigh 1902, Sadler 1917, Hartog 1929, Wardha 1937, Sargent 1944, Radhakrishnan 1948, Kothari 1964**. The first four are British-era bodies. Wardha is Gandhi's. The last two belong to free India.

= eia
Q Who founded the East India Association, where and in which year?
A Dadabhai Naoroji, London, 1866
S Mr Narrow Majority | **Dadabhai Naoroji** believed the British public would be fair if only it knew the truth about India. So he went to live among them. In **1866** he started the **East India Association** in London. In **1892** he stood for the British Parliament from Finsbury and won by a handful of votes, so few that people joked and called him "Mr Narrow Majority". He was the **first Indian member of the House of Commons**. India called him the **Grand Old Man**.
Q Who was the first Indian elected to the British House of Commons?
A Dadabhai Naoroji
N He won in 1892. He is called the Grand Old Man of India.

= hicky
Q Which was the first newspaper printed in India, and in which year?
A Hicky's Bengal Gazette, 1780
S The first editor went to jail | India's first newspaper was a small weekly started at Calcutta in **1780** by an Irishman, **James Augustus Hicky**. He called it the **Bengal Gazette**. Hicky printed gossip about the Company's officers and then went after the Governor-General himself, **Warren Hastings**. He was fined and jailed, and in 1782 his types were seized. The paper lived only two years. But it set the pattern for a hundred years: Indian papers and the government were never friends.

= presslaws
Q Which Governor-General brought in press censorship in 1799?
A Lord Wellesley
Q Who is called the Liberator of the Indian Press, and why?
A Charles Metcalfe; he removed the press curbs in 1835
Q Who made a licence compulsory for every press in 1823?
A John Adam
F Four press laws | **1799** Wellesley brings censorship, in fear of the French. **1823** John Adam makes a licence compulsory. **1835** Charles Metcalfe frees the press. **1878** Lytton gags the Indian-language papers. Tight, tighter, free, tight again.
