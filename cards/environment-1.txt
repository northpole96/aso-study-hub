# environment | Environment

## env-words

= ecology
Q Who coined the word "ecology"?
A Ernst Haeckel
Q Who coined the word "ecosystem", and in which year?
A A. G. Tansley, 1935
Q Who drew the first pyramid of numbers?
A Charles Elton
F Three words, three people | **Haeckel** named the subject: **ecology**, the study of living things and their surroundings. **Tansley** named the unit: the **ecosystem**, in 1935. **Elton** drew its shape: the **pyramid of numbers**, with many plants at the bottom and very few hunters at the top.

= tenpercent
Q Who gave the 10 per cent law of energy flow, and in which year?
A Raymond Lindeman, 1942
Q Under the 10 per cent law, how much energy passes from one food level to the next?
A About 10%
N The rest is lost, mostly as heat.
Q Which ecological pyramid is always upright and can never be inverted?
A The pyramid of energy
N A pyramid of numbers or biomass can be upside down; energy cannot.
F Why the pyramid narrows | At each step up the food chain only about **10%** of the energy passes on. Grass with 1,000 units feeds deer with 100, and the deer feed a tiger with 10. That is why there are so few top hunters, and why the **pyramid of energy is always upright**. **Lindeman** gave the law in **1942**.

= diversity
Q Who gave the terms alpha, beta and gamma diversity?
A R. H. Whittaker
Q What does beta diversity measure?
A The change in species from one community to the next
N Asked in 2021. Alpha is inside one community; gamma is the whole region.
Q What is alpha diversity?
A The variety of species inside one community
Q What is gamma diversity?
A The total variety of species over a whole region
F Alpha, beta, gamma | Stand in one pond and count: that is **alpha**. Walk from the pond into the forest and see how the species change: that is **beta**. Count everything in the whole region: that is **gamma**. Beta is the one the examiner asks, because it is about **change between** communities.

= hotspot
Q Who gave the idea of the biodiversity hotspot, and in which year?
A Norman Myers, 1988
N Asked in 2021.
Q How many endemic plant species must a region have to be a biodiversity hotspot?
A At least 1,500
Q How much of its original vegetation must a hotspot have lost?
A At least 70%
Q How many biodiversity hotspots are there in the world?
A 36
Q Name the four biodiversity hotspots that touch India.
A Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats with Sri Lanka, Sundaland
N Sundaland reaches India through the Nicobar Islands.
F Rich and in danger | A hotspot must pass two tests. It must be **rich**: at least **1,500 endemic plants**. And it must be **in danger**: at least **70% of its vegetation lost**. **Norman Myers** gave the idea in **1988**. The world has **36**; India touches **four**.
S A list of places to save first | In **1988** a British ecologist, **Norman Myers**, asked a hard question. There is not enough money to save every forest. Which ones should come first? His answer was the places where many plants live nowhere else and where most of the land has already been cleared. He called them **biodiversity hotspots**. The idea caught on, and the list has grown to **36**. Four of them reach into India, from the Himalaya to the **Nicobar Islands**.

= cops
Q At which COP was the Paris Agreement made, and in which year?
A COP21, 2015
Q What warming limit did the Paris Agreement set?
A Well below 2°C
Q Where was COP30 held in 2025?
A Belém, Brazil
Q Where was COP31 held in 2026?
A Antalya, Türkiye
F The climate road | **Rio 1992**: the climate convention is signed. **Kyoto 1997**: the first binding cuts, in force 2005. **Paris 2015**, COP21: keep warming **well below 2°C**. **Belém 2025**: COP30. **Antalya 2026**: COP31. A COP is the yearly meeting of the countries that signed at Rio.
S The town that gave its name to a disease | In the 1950s the cats of **Minamata**, a fishing town in Japan, began to shake and fall into the sea. Then the people fell ill. A factory had been pouring waste full of **mercury** into the bay for years, and the fish carried it to every table. The illness became known as **Minamata disease**. In 2013 the world signed a treaty to control mercury, and it was named after the town.

= parks

F One park, one animal | **Kaziranga**, Assam: the **one-horned rhino**. **Gir**, Gujarat: the **Asiatic lion**. **Dachigam**, near Srinagar: the **hangul**. **Keibul Lamjao**, Manipur: the **sangai**, on floating islands of Loktak Lake. S The park that floats | In Manipur lies **Loktak Lake**, covered with floating mats of soil and plants called phumdis. On these mats lives a deer found nowhere else on earth, the **sangai**. It walks on the soft ground with a careful step, which is why it is called the dancing deer. To save it, India made **Keibul Lamjao**, the only floating national park in the world.

## env-watchers

= cpcb
D 1974 | Water Act and the CPCB | Parliament passed the **Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act** and created the **Central Pollution Control Board** to enforce it. Its duties over air came with the Air Act of 1981.
Q Under which Act was the Central Pollution Control Board set up?
A Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974
Q In which year was the Air (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act passed?
A 1981
F Born from water | The **CPCB** was not made for the air. It was born from the **Water Act of 1974**, to keep rivers clean. The **Air Act of 1981** later gave it the air too. The examiner offers 1981 or the Environment Act as the parent. The answer is the Water Act.

= epa
D 1986 | Environment (Protection) Act | After the **Bhopal gas leak** of December 1984, Parliament passed a wide law that lets the Centre act on **any kind of pollution**. It is an umbrella over the older water and air laws.
Q Which disaster led to the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986?
A Bhopal gas leak, 1984
Q In which year was the Wild Life (Protection) Act passed?
A 1972
Q In which year was the National Green Tribunal set up?
A 2010
S The night in Bhopal | On a December night in **1984**, a gas leaked from a pesticide factory in **Bhopal**. It spread over the sleeping city, and thousands died. India had laws for water and for air, but none wide enough for a disaster like this. Two years later Parliament passed the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**, which lets the Centre act against any kind of pollution, from any source.

= poisons
Q Minamata disease is caused by which metal?
A Mercury
Q Itai-itai disease is caused by which metal?
A Cadmium

F Three poisons, three names | **Minamata** disease: **mercury**, from fish in a poisoned bay in Japan. **Itai-itai**, "it hurts, it hurts": **cadmium**, which makes bones soft and painful. **Blue baby syndrome**: **nitrates** in well water, which stop a baby's blood from carrying oxygen.
