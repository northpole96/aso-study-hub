# polity | The Constitution

## pol-money

= budget
Q What does the Constitution call the budget, and under which Article?
A Annual Financial Statement; Article 112
Q What is a Vote on Account?
A An advance grant to run the government until the budget is passed
N Asked in 2018. It usually covers two months.
Q Which Bill allows the government to take money out of the Consolidated Fund of India?
A Appropriation Bill
F Three purses | The Union keeps its money in three places. The **Consolidated Fund** holds all taxes and loans; nothing leaves it without a law of Parliament. The **Public Account** holds money that belongs to others, such as provident funds. The **Contingency Fund** is a small purse for emergencies, in the hands of the President.

= moneybill
Q Which Article defines a Money Bill?
A Article 110
Q In which House must a Money Bill be introduced?
A Lok Sabha
N And only on the recommendation of the President.
Q Who decides whether a Bill is a Money Bill?
A The Speaker of the Lok Sabha
N The Speaker's decision is final.
Q For how many days can the Rajya Sabha keep a Money Bill?
A 14 days
Q What can the Lok Sabha do with changes the Rajya Sabha suggests to a Money Bill?
A Accept or reject them
N Asked in 2025. The Rajya Sabha can only recommend.
Q Can there be a joint sitting of the two Houses on a Money Bill?
A No
F Why the Rajya Sabha is weak on money | The Lok Sabha is elected directly by the people who pay the taxes. So on a **Money Bill** the Rajya Sabha can neither reject nor change it. It can only suggest, and it must send the Bill back within **14 days**. If it sits on the Bill, the Bill is taken as passed.

= joint
Q Under which Article is a joint sitting of the two Houses held, and who presides?
A Article 108; the Speaker of the Lok Sabha
N A joint sitting has been held only three times.

= cuts
Q A policy cut motion reduces a demand to how much?
A One rupee
N It says: we reject the whole policy behind this demand.
Q A token cut motion reduces a demand by how much?
A One hundred rupees
N It is used to air one particular complaint.
F Three cut motions | **Policy cut**: reduce the demand **to ₹1**; we reject the policy. **Economy cut**: reduce it **by a stated sum**; you are spending too much. **Token cut**: reduce it **by ₹100**; we have one complaint. Watch the small words "to" and "by".

= cag
Q Under which Article is the Comptroller and Auditor General appointed, and by whom?
A Article 148; the President
Q What is the term of office of the CAG?
A Six years or till the age of 65
Q To whom does the CAG submit his reports on the Union's accounts?
A The President
N The President places them before Parliament. For a State, the reports go to the Governor.
Q How can the CAG be removed from office?
A In the same way as a judge of the Supreme Court
Q Who is the present Comptroller and Auditor General of India?
A K. Sanjay Murthy
F The guardian of the public purse | The **CAG** checks every rupee the Centre and the States spend. To keep him free, the Constitution gives him a fixed term (**six years or 65**), makes him as hard to remove as a Supreme Court judge, and bars him from any government job afterwards. Ambedkar called him the most important officer under the Constitution.

= pac
Q How many members does the Public Accounts Committee have, and from which Houses?
A 22: 15 from the Lok Sabha and 7 from the Rajya Sabha
Q Who is called the "friend, philosopher and guide" of the Public Accounts Committee?
A The Comptroller and Auditor General
Q By convention, the chairman of the Public Accounts Committee comes from which side?
A The Opposition
N The Speaker appoints him. A minister cannot be a member.
Q How many members does the Estimates Committee have, and from which House?
A 30, all from the Lok Sabha
F Two committees on money | The **Public Accounts Committee** (22 members, both Houses) looks back: was the money spent as Parliament ordered? It works from the CAG's reports. The **Estimates Committee** (30 members, Lok Sabha only) looks forward: could the same work be done for less?

= fincom
Q Under which Article is the Finance Commission set up, and how often?
A Article 280; every five years
N It advises how taxes should be shared between the Centre and the States.
Q Who chairs the Sixteenth Finance Commission?
A Arvind Panagariya

## pol-state

= governor
Q Who appoints the Governor of a State, and under which Article?
A The President; Article 155
Q For how long does a Governor hold office?
A During the pleasure of the President
N The normal term is five years, but the President can remove or transfer a Governor at any time.
Q Can a Governor be impeached?
A No
N Only the President can be impeached. The State Assembly has no power to remove a Governor.
Q What is the minimum age to be a Governor?
A 35 years
Q Which amendment allowed one person to be Governor of two or more States?
A 7th Amendment, 1956
Q Who gives the oath of office to a Governor?
A The Chief Justice of the High Court
F Elected by nobody | The Governor is the head of the State, yet no one in the State votes for him. The **President appoints** him (Article 155) and he stays at the **President's pleasure** (Article 156). The makers copied this from Canada, because they feared an elected Governor would clash with the elected Chief Minister.

= sarkaria
Q Which commission said that a Governor should be a non-partisan person from outside the State?
A Sarkaria Commission
N Asked in 2025. It was set up in 1983 to study Centre–State relations.
Q Which later commission, set up in 2007, also studied Centre–State relations?
A Punchhi Commission

= odgov
Q Who was named Governor of Odisha in October 2026?
A Braj Mohan Ram
N He followed Hari Babu Kambhampati.
Q In which city is the High Court of Odisha, and in which year was it set up?
A Cuttack; 1948
Q How many seats does Odisha have in the State Assembly, the Lok Sabha and the Rajya Sabha?
A 147, 21 and 10
F A minister Odisha must have | Article 164 says that in four States there must be a **Minister in charge of tribal welfare**: **Odisha**, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand. The same Article says the Governor appoints the Chief Minister, and the other ministers on the Chief Minister's advice.
Q Article 164 says four States must have a Minister for tribal welfare. Name them.
A Odisha, Madhya Pradesh, Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand

= council
Q How many States have a Legislative Council today?
A Six
Q Name the six States that have a Legislative Council.
A Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra and Karnataka
Q Does Odisha have a Legislative Council?
A No
N The Odisha Assembly asked for one in 2018, but Parliament has not made the law.
Q Under which Article is a Legislative Council created or abolished, and by whom?
A Article 169; Parliament
N First the State Assembly must pass a resolution by a special majority. Asked in 2021.
Q What are the largest and smallest sizes allowed for a Legislative Council?
A One-third of the Assembly; at least 40 members
F Who sits in a Council | A Legislative Council is filled in five ways. **One-third** are chosen by the MLAs. **One-third** by local bodies. **One-twelfth** by graduates. **One-twelfth** by teachers. The last **one-sixth** are named by the Governor. A member sits for six years.
S The House that Odisha asked for | Odisha has one House, the Assembly of 147 members. In **2018** that Assembly passed a resolution asking for a second House, a Legislative Council. Under **Article 169** that is only the first step. The second step belongs to **Parliament**, which must pass a law to create the Council. Parliament has not done so. So the resolution lies in Delhi, and Odisha remains one of the twenty-two States with a single House.

= ages
Q What is the minimum age to be a member of the Lok Sabha or a State Assembly?
A 25 years
Q What is the minimum age to be a member of the Rajya Sabha or a Legislative Council?
A 30 years
N Asked in 2025.
Q What is the minimum age to be a member of a Panchayat or a Municipality?
A 21 years
Q What is the minimum age to be President or Vice-President of India?
A 35 years
F The ladder of ages | **18** to vote. **21** for a Panchayat or Municipality. **25** for the Lok Sabha or an Assembly. **30** for the Rajya Sabha or a Council. **35** for President, Vice-President or Governor.

= powers
Q Under which Article does a Governor issue ordinances, and under which does the President?
A Article 213 (Governor); Article 123 (President)
Q Under which Article does the Governor have the power to pardon, and under which does the President?
A Article 161 (Governor); Article 72 (President)
Q Who is the highest law officer of a State, and under which Article?
A Advocate General; Article 165
N The Centre's law officer is the Attorney General, under Article 76.

## pol-watchdogs

= psc
Q Which Articles deal with the Public Service Commissions?
A Articles 315 to 323
Q Who appoints the chairman and members of a State Public Service Commission?
A The Governor
Q Who can remove the chairman or a member of a State Public Service Commission?
A Only the President
N The Governor appoints but cannot remove. For misbehaviour, the Supreme Court must first hold an inquiry.
Q What is the term of a member of the UPSC?
A Six years or till the age of 65
Q What is the term of a member of a State Public Service Commission?
A Six years or till the age of 62
Q Who creates a Joint Public Service Commission for two or more States?
A Parliament
N Asked in 2025. It acts when the State Legislatures ask for it.
Q After retiring, can the chairman of the UPSC take another job under the government?
A No
N Asked in 2021. A State chairman can still become chairman or member of the UPSC.
F Appointed here, removed there | The **Governor** appoints the members of a State Commission, but only the **President** can remove them. This split is on purpose. A State government that dislikes the Commission's choice of officers cannot punish it.
F UPSC and State PSC side by side | **Appointed by**: President, Governor. **Removed by**: President in both. **Term**: six years in both. **Age limit**: 65 for the UPSC, **62** for a State Commission. **Report goes to**: President, Governor.

= opsc
D 1949 | Odisha Public Service Commission | The body that holds the ASO examination was set up on **1 April 1949**. Its office is at **Cuttack**. It works under Articles 315 to 323, like every State Commission.
Q In which year was the Odisha Public Service Commission set up, and where is its office?
A 1949; Cuttack
Q In which year was the first Public Service Commission set up in India?
A 1926
N The Lee Commission had recommended it in 1924.

= nhrc
D 1993 | National Human Rights Commission | It was set up under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**. So it is a **statutory** body, made by a law, and not a constitutional one.
Q Under which Act was the National Human Rights Commission set up?
A Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993
Q Who was the first chairperson of the National Human Rights Commission?
A Justice Ranganath Misra
N Asked in 2021. He was born in Odisha and had been Chief Justice of India.
Q Since 2019, who can be the chairperson of the NHRC?
A A former Chief Justice of India or a former Supreme Court judge
N Before 2019 only a former Chief Justice of India could hold the post.
Q What is the term of the NHRC chairperson since 2019?
A Three years or till the age of 70
N It was five years before the change.
Q Who is the present chairperson of the NHRC?
A Justice V. Ramasubramanian
S The Odia judge in the first chair | When the National Human Rights Commission opened in **1993**, its first chairperson was **Justice Ranganath Misra**. He was born at Banpur in Odisha and began as a lawyer in Cuttack. He rose to be a judge of the Orissa High Court, then of the Supreme Court, and in 1990 the **Chief Justice of India**. In those days only a former Chief Justice could head the Commission. That rule was relaxed in **2019**.

= nhrcpanel
Q How many members sit on the committee that recommends the NHRC chairperson, and who heads it?
A Six; the Prime Minister
Q Name the six members of the committee that selects the NHRC chairperson.
A PM, Speaker, Home Minister, both Leaders of the Opposition, Deputy Chairman of Rajya Sabha
N The Deputy Speaker of the Lok Sabha is not on it. That was the trap in 2025.
Q Who appoints the chairperson of the NHRC?
A The President

= eci

A Article 324
Q What is the term of the Chief Election Commissioner?
A Six years or till the age of 65
Q How can the Chief Election Commissioner be removed?
A In the same way as a judge of the Supreme Court
Q Who was the first Chief Election Commissioner of India?
A Sukumar Sen

A 25 January; the Election Commission was founded on that day in 1950

= kinds
F In the book or made by a law? | **Constitutional** bodies have their own Article: the Election Commission (324), UPSC (315), Finance Commission (280), CAG (148). **Statutory** bodies were made by an Act: the NHRC, the Central Information Commission, the Lokpal. **NITI Aayog** is neither. It was made by a Cabinet resolution.
Q Is the National Human Rights Commission a constitutional body or a statutory body?
A A statutory body
Q NITI Aayog was created by which means?
A A resolution of the Union Cabinet
N So it is neither constitutional nor statutory. It replaced the Planning Commission in 2015.
