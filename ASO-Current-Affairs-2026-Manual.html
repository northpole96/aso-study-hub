I read every current-affairs question in the 2018, 2021 and 2025 ASO General Awareness papers, grouped them into families, and then researched what happened in each family from late 2025 to November 2026. Each section shows what was asked before, explains the 2026 news in plain words, and ends with "likely next" cards you can flip.

The paper does not ask all the news. It asks the same few kinds again and again: exercises and missiles, days and their themes, schemes, Odisha, summits, and India's rank in reports. If you know those kinds well, most of the other news can be skipped.

Section 00 · BEFORE YOU READ

How the ASO paper asks current affairs

In all three papers, about 30 of the 100 questions come from the news. They are not all in one block. In 2021 the first 20 questions were "current affairs", but news also appeared inside Defence (Sela tunnel, the Air Chief, the naval helicopter), International Organisations (the SCO and BIMSTEC hosts, the new UN Security Council member) and Environment (Jeypore rainforest). In 2025 the news questions were spread through the whole paper.

The most useful finding is about timing. The 2025 paper was written on 9 November 2025, but its newest news items are from May and June 2025: Operation Keller, the PSLV-C61 launch, the US "Golden Dome", Desert Flag-10 and Nomadic Elephant. Nothing from July to November 2025 was asked. So the paper is set about five months before the exam. For a 17 January 2027 exam, the most likely window is roughly September 2025 to August 2026. Learn September to December 2026 too, but lightly.

The paper also reaches back. The 2025 paper asked the theme of Earth Day 2024, not 2025. So for every "theme" question, learn the last two years, not just this one.

Section 01 · DEFENCE AND SECURITY

Defence: exercises, missiles, ships, chiefs

This is the biggest family. In 2025 alone there were nine defence questions with a news angle. The pattern is simple: a named exercise or operation, and you must know the partner country, the place, the edition number, and one feature.

2026 was an unusual year for the top of the armed forces. All four top posts changed hands. The Chief of Defence Staff and the Navy chief changed on 31 May, the Army chief on 30 June, and the Air chief on 31 October. A question on "the present Chief of ___" (asked in 2021, Q82) is very likely.

Section 02 · DAYS AND THEMES

Days and their themes

Theme questions appear in every paper. Earth Day alone came three times. The 2025 paper asked the 2024 Earth Day theme, so the safe habit is: for each day, learn the date, the 2025 theme and the 2026 theme. The options often contain the themes of nearby years, so knowing two years helps you cut wrong options.

Themes of UN days are set by the UN body in charge (UNEP for Environment Day, UN-Water for Water Day, WHO for Health Day). Indian days are set by the ministry or body in charge (the Election Commission for Voters' Day).

Section 03 · BUDGET, SCHEMES, ECONOMY

Budget, schemes and the economy

Past papers asked one unusual word from the budget ("Virtual Digital Assets"), which state made a child budget, the current repo rate, and which ministry or body runs a scheme. So for the 2026-27 budget, learn the headline numbers and any new names. For schemes, learn the ministry and the target group.

2026 also changed how India measures its economy. GDP now uses 2022-23 as its base year (in place of 2011-12), and retail inflation (CPI) uses 2024 (in place of 2012). A base year is the "reference year" whose prices are used to compare all later years, so changing it changes the growth numbers.

Section 06 · SUMMITS AND WORLD BODIES

Summits and world bodies

The question is nearly always "where was it held" or "who hosted it", sometimes "which number summit" or "who is the new member". In 2021 OPSC asked who hosted the SCO and BIMSTEC summits and which country joined the UN Security Council. Learn the host and the number for every summit below.

India chaired BRICS in 2026, so the BRICS summit is the single most likely summit question.

The 2026 Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz

On 28 February 2026 the US and Israel launched major strikes on Iran's military and nuclear sites. Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz to shipping. After about 40 days, Pakistan brokered a two-week ceasefire on 8 April 2026, but fighting and closures returned. The US then blockaded Iranian ports. On 14–15 June 2026 the US and Iran agreed a framework to end the war and reopen the strait in stages.

For India this meant high oil prices (one reason the RBI raised rates in October), the Navy's Operation Urja Suraksha to escort ships, and debate at the BRICS summit, where members disagreed about the war. Learn the outline; exact casualty or leadership claims vary between sources.

India in space

PSLV-C62 failed (12 January 2026)
Carried DRDO's EOS-N1 "Anvesha" (a maritime surveillance satellite) and 15 small satellites. A problem near the end of the third stage meant none reached orbit. It was the PSLV's second failure in a row after PSLV-C61 / EOS-09 (18 May 2025).

World space

Launched on the SLS rocket in the Orion capsule; splashed down in the Pacific. Flew farther from Earth than any humans before, beating Apollo 13's record.
