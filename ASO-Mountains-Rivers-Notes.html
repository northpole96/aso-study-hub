MOUNTAINS AND RIVERS.

India and Odisha, with the world records worth knowing. Hills, passes, rivers, dams and the traps OPSC sets.

· · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · · ·

India's land tells two stories. In the north stand the Himalayas, young mountains that are still rising, and their rivers are fed by snow, so they flow all year. In the south lies the Peninsula, an old and worn block of hard rock, and its rivers are fed only by rain, so many run thin in summer.

Odisha sits where the Eastern Ghats meet the sea. Its hills hold the minerals, and its rivers build the coastal plain. That is why OPSC asks two to five Odisha land questions in every paper. These notes follow the official syllabus line "Physical Geography of India & Odisha", with a short world page from line (e). Each part ends with Odisha, then the past questions and what is likely next.

01 · Mountains · India

The Himalayas.

The Himalayas are young fold mountains. "Fold" means they were made when the Indian plate pushed into the Eurasian plate and the rock layers crumpled up like a rug pushed against a wall. They run about 2,400 km in an arc from the Indus in the west to the Brahmaputra in the east. Think of them as parallel belts, and learn them from the plains upward: the lowest belt is nearest to you, the highest is farthest.

02 · Mountains · India

Mountain passes.

A pass is a low gap in a range that people can cross. OPSC asked a "which pass is in the wrong state?" question in 2021, so learn every pass with its state, and with what it joins. The easy way is to walk the border from west to east: Ladakh, Himachal, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Arunachal. Many of these passes are also in defence news, because they face China.

Passes in the peninsula

The Western Ghats are a near-unbroken wall, so the few gaps carry the roads and railways from the coast inland. Thal Ghat joins Mumbai and Nashik, Bhor Ghat joins Mumbai and Pune, and Pal Ghat (Palakkad Gap) joins Kerala and Tamil Nadu, between the Nilgiris and the Anaimalai. Further south, the Shencottah Gap crosses into Kerala near Kollam.

03 · Mountains · India

Peninsular ranges.

The ranges of the south are old. They are not new folds but worn stumps of very old mountains (residual mountains) or the raised edges of broken blocks of land (block mountains). That is why they are low and round-topped. OPSC asked in 2018 where the oldest rocks are: the answer was the Aravallis.

04 · Mountains · Odisha

Odisha hills.

Odisha has four land regions: the northern plateau, the Eastern Ghats hills, the central tableland, and the coastal plain. The hills run from Mayurbhanj in the north to Koraput in the south. Learn each hill with its district and the one story attached to it, because OPSC questions always come with that story: a god, a tribe, a mineral or a court case.

05 · Rivers · India

Himalayan rivers.

Himalayan rivers are perennial: they flow all year, because they get melting snow in summer and rain in the monsoon. They are older than the mountains themselves (antecedent rivers), so they cut deep gorges through them. In the plains they meander and build huge flat plains. There are three systems: the Indus, the Ganga and the Brahmaputra.

Indus system

The Indus rises in Tibet near Lake Mansarovar, flows through Ladakh, and turns south into Pakistan. Its five eastern tributaries give Punjab its name ("five waters"): Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej, joining it on the left bank. The Jhelum rises at Verinag and passes through Wular Lake; the Chenab forms where the Chandra and Bhaga meet at Tandi; the Sutlej rises at Rakas Tal and enters India at Shipki La; the Bhakra Nangal dam is on it. Under the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) India got the three eastern rivers (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) and Pakistan the three western ones (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab). India put the treaty "in abeyance" in April 2025 after the Pahalgam attack.

Ganga system

The Ganga (2,525 km) is the longest river in India. It is formed by two streams: the Bhagirathi from the Gangotri glacier (Gaumukh) and the Alaknanda, which meet at Devprayag. On the Alaknanda lie the five "prayags" (meeting points): Vishnuprayag, Nandprayag, Karnaprayag, Rudraprayag and Devprayag. The Ganga is India's national river.

The Yamuna rises at Yamunotri and is the Ganga's longest tributary. In Bangladesh the Ganga is called the Padma; it joins the Brahmaputra and then the Meghna to build the Sundarbans delta, the largest delta in the world.

Brahmaputra system

The Brahmaputra rises from the Chemayungdung glacier near Mansarovar. It has three names: Tsangpo in Tibet, Siang or Dihang in Arunachal, and Jamuna in Bangladesh. In Assam it joins the Dibang and the Lohit and flows as a very wide, braided river. Majuli in Assam is the largest river island in India. Right-bank tributaries: Subansiri, Kameng (Jia Bharali), Manas, Sankosh. Left-bank: Dibang, Lohit, Burhi Dihing, Dhansiri.

06 · Rivers · India

Peninsular rivers.

Peninsular rivers are seasonal: they are fed by rain only, so they shrink in summer. They flow in old, wide, shallow valleys. Because the Western Ghats are near the west coast, the main water-divide is in the west, and most big rivers flow east into the Bay of Bengal, where they build deltas. Only two big rivers, the Narmada and the Tapi, flow west into the Arabian Sea. They run in rift valleys (long cracks where land has sunk), move fast, and form estuaries instead of deltas.

07 · Rivers · Odisha

Odisha rivers, dams and falls.

Odisha's coastal plain is a "hexadeltaic" plain: it is built by the deltas of six rivers. From north to south: Subarnarekha, Budhabalanga, Baitarani, Brahmani, Mahanadi and Rushikulya. OPSC asked what "hexadeltaic" means in 2025, and asked the order of their share in 2021 (Mahanadi largest, Rushikulya smallest). In the south, other rivers rise in Odisha but flow out to the Godavari or into Andhra.

The Mahanadi

The Mahanadi (about 851 km) rises near Sihawa in Dhamtari, Chhattisgarh, so its upper basin is in Chhattisgarh, and the two states have a water dispute (a tribunal was set up in 2018). Its main tributaries are the Seonath (its longest, in Chhattisgarh), the Hasdeo, Mand, Ib and Tel. The Hirakud Dam near Sambalpur (opened 1957) is one of the longest earthen dams in the world, and its reservoir is a Ramsar site (2021). Cuttack stands at the head of the delta, where the river splits into the Kathajodi, Birupa, Devi, Daya and Bhargavi. It reaches the sea near Paradip (False Point).

Falls and lakes

The highest waterfall in Odisha is Barehipani in Similipal (about 399 m) Check; Joranda is close by. Khandadhar (Sundargarh) and Duduma (Koraput) are the other big ones. Chilika is a lagoon: a shallow body of salty water cut off from the sea by a sandbar that the waves built. It is Asia's largest brackish-water lagoon, fed by the Mahanadi branches Daya and Bhargavi, and it was India's first Ramsar site in 1981 (with Keoladeo). 2018 Q45

08 · Syllabus line (e) · World

World mountains and rivers.

Line (e) of the syllabus, "World Physical Geography", is asked as records and odd facts: the river that crosses the equator twice (2025), the river with a bird-foot delta (2021). Learn each record with its rivals, because the wrong options are always the rivals.

Highest peak
Everest 8,849 m (Nepal–China). Second: K2.
Longest range
Andes (South America, ~7,000 km). Highest there: Aconcagua.
Continent tops
Kilimanjaro (Africa), Denali (N. America), Elbrus (Europe), Vinson (Antarctica).
Young fold ranges
Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas. Old: Appalachian, Urals, Aravalli.
Largest plateau
Tibet, "the roof of the world".
Longest river
Nile (~6,650 km). Largest by water flow: Amazon.
Equator twice
Congo, also the deepest river. 2025 Q80
Bird-foot delta
Mississippi. The Nile's delta is arc-shaped. 2021 Q66
Most countries
Danube, through 10 countries.
Lakes
Largest: Caspian Sea. Deepest and oldest: Baikal. Largest freshwater: Superior.

10 · Last week

Memory hooks.

  1. Four highest: Kanchenjunga in India, Nanda Devi wholly in India, Anamudi in the peninsula, Deomali in Odisha.
  2. Himalaya from the plains up: Shiwalik → Himachal → Himadri → Trans-Himalaya. "Shy Him Hides Tibet."
  3. Passes west to east: Ladakh (Karakoram, Khardung, Zoji) → J&K (Banihal) → Himachal (Rohtang, Bara-lacha, Shipki) → Uttarakhand (Mana, Niti, Lipulekh) → Sikkim (Nathu, Jelep) → Arunachal (Bom Di, Se, Bum, Diphu).
  4. Southern hills, north to south: Nilgiri, Anaimalai, Palani, Cardamom. "No Ant Picks Cardamom."
  5. West-flowing pair: Narmada and Tapi, rift valleys, estuaries. Everything big else goes east and makes a delta.
  6. Amarkantak gives two: Narmada and Son. Not the Tapi (Multai), not the Mahanadi (Sihawa).
  7. Six deltas of Odisha, north to south: Subarnarekha, Budhabalanga, Baitarani, Brahmani, Mahanadi, Rushikulya.
  8. Three turtle beaches: Gahirmatha, Rushikulya, Devi mouth.
  9. Mahanadi in one line: Sihawa → Hirakud (Sambalpur) → Cuttack (delta head) → Paradip.
