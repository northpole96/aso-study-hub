Chapter 01 · WEAK SPOT · Q3, Q77, Q81

1. Tense signals

In a tense question, one time word decides the answer. Find it before you look at the options.

FIG_001Each tense is a place on one line. When two past actions are given, the earlier one takes had + V3.

Signal wordUseExample
had + V3 in the if-part, today / now in the resultwould + base verbIf you had taken the medicine yesterday, you would feel better today.
had + V3 in the if-part, result also pastwould have + V3…you would have felt better yesterday.
This is the first / third timehas / have + V3This is the third time she has won.
It was the first timehad + V3It was the first time I had flown.
as if / as though (not true)past form; were for beShe acts as if she were the owner.

Q3 · you chose: would have felt

If you had taken the medicine on time yesterday, you ____ much better today.

  • would feel ✓ right
  • would have felt ✗ your pick
  • will feel
  • had felt

Why: today puts the result in the present, so would + base verb.

Q77 · you chose: wins

This is the third time she ____ the state-level debate competition.

  • has won ✓ right
  • won
  • had won
  • wins ✗ your pick

Why: "This is the third time" counts up to now, so present perfect.

Q81 · you chose: is

She gives orders to everyone as if she ____ the owner of the house.

  • were ✓ right
  • is ✗ your pick
  • has been
  • will be

Why: She is not the owner. An untrue comparison takes were.

Check yourself

"If I had saved money last year, I ____ worried now." (would not be / would not have been)

would not be. The result is now.

"This is the second time you ____ late." (are / have been)

have been.

"He spends as though he ____ a millionaire." (is / were)

were.

Likely next: "It is high time we ____ the rules." (change / changed)

changed.

Chapter 02 · WEAK SPOT · Q4

2. "Only when" at the start

When Only when, Only after, Only then, Not until, Never, Seldom, Hardly, No sooner opens a sentence, the main clause takes question order: helping verb before the subject. If there is no helping verb, add do / does / did. The 2025 paper asked this four times 2025 Q8 2025 Q40.

✗ WrongOnly when the lights came back on we saw the damage.
✓ RightOnly when the lights came back on did we see the damage.
✓ RightOnly after the doctor checked him was he allowed home.

The "when" part keeps normal order. Only the main part turns round.

Q4 · you chose: we saw

Only when the lights came back on ____ how much damage the storm had done to the hall.

  • did we see ✓ right
  • we saw ✗ your pick
  • we did see
  • saw we

Why: The sentence opens with Only when, so the main part turns round.

Check yourself

"Not until he lost his job ____ the value of money." (he realised / did he realise)

did he realise.

"Seldom ____ such a calm sea." (we see / do we see)

do we see.

Likely next: "Under no circumstances ____ leave the hall." (candidates should / should candidates)

should candidates.

Chapter 03 · WEAK SPOT · Q12, Q19, Q29

3. would rather, deny, lest

These words fix the shape of the next verb. Learn each as a frame.

FrameRuleExample
would rather + you / he / theypast formI would rather you did not mention it.
would rather (my own action)base verbI would rather stay home.
deny, admit, avoid, mind, enjoy, finish, risk+ -ing (never "to")He denied having been there.
lest (= so that … not)+ should + base verb; no extra "not"Walk carefully lest you should fall.

Q12 · you chose: not mention

I would rather you ____ this matter to anyone until the enquiry is over.

  • did not mention ✓ right
  • do not mention
  • not mention ✗ your pick
  • had not mentioned

Why: The speaker means another person (you), so the past form.

Q19 · you chose: to be

The accused denied ____ anywhere near the bank on the night of the robbery.

  • having been ✓ right
  • to have been
  • to be ✗ your pick
  • have been

Why: Deny takes -ing, never "to".

Q29 · you skipped it

Choose the grammatically correct sentence:

  • Walk carefully lest you should fall. ✓ right
  • Walk carefully lest you should not fall.
  • Walk carefully lest you will fall.
  • Walk carefully lest you may not fall.

Why: Lest already means "so that … not": add should, never another "not".

Check yourself

"My mother would rather I ____ home before dark." (come / came)

came.

"She avoided ____ his eyes." (to meet / meeting)

meeting.

"Run fast lest you ____ the bus." (miss / should miss / will miss)

should miss.

Likely next: "Would you mind ____ the window?" (to open / opening)

opening.

Chapter 04 · WEAK SPOT · Q10, Q14

4. No improvement; "the reason is that"

Two opposite lessons from sentence improvement 2021 Q12 2021 Q13.

4.1Lesson 1: sometimes nothing is wrong

Try the sentence as it is first. If it is correct and every option breaks it, choose No improvement. The frame "The more …, the less …" needs the in both halves and the plain word less.

✓ RightThe more you practise, the less nervous you will feel.
✗ WrongThe more you practise, less nervous you will feel.

4.2Lesson 2: don't say "reason" twice

Reason and because mean the same thing. Write the reason is that.

✗ WrongThe reason he failed is because he did not read the questions.
✓ RightThe reason he failed is that he did not read the questions.

Also: because of + noun (because of his illness); because + clause (because he was ill).

Q10 · you chose: less nervous you will feel

Improve the part in brackets, or choose No improvement:

The more you practise speaking in public, (the less nervous you will feel) on the stage.

  • less nervous you will feel ✗ your pick
  • the lesser nervous you will feel
  • the less nervous will you feel
  • No improvement ✓ right

Why: The sentence was already right: the in both halves, plain less, normal order.

Q14 · you chose: was because

Improve the part in brackets, or choose No improvement:

The reason why he failed the test (is because) he did not read the questions carefully.

  • was because ✗ your pick
  • is that ✓ right
  • is due to
  • No improvement

Why: Reason + because says the same thing twice. The fault was "because", not the tense.

Check yourself

Improve: "The higher you climb, (more colder) it becomes."

the colder.

Improve: "(Though he is poor, but) he is honest."

Though he is poor, — drop "but".

Likely next: "He could not come (because of he was ill)."

because he was ill.

Chapter 05 · WEAK SPOT · Q28

5. who, with "I thought" in the middle

Who does the action (subject). Whom receives it (object). When I thought, we believe, they said sits in the middle, cover it and read again.

The man who/whom I thought was honest → "the man who was honest". So who.

✗ WrongThe man whom I thought was honest cheated me.
✓ RightThe man who I thought was honest cheated me.
✓ RightThe man whom I trusted cheated me. (I trusted him: object)

Q28 · you chose: The man whom I thought was honest cheated me.

Choose the grammatically correct sentence:

  • The man who I thought was honest cheated me. ✓ right
  • The man whom I thought was honest cheated me. ✗ your pick
  • The man which I thought was honest cheated me.
  • The man who I thought he was honest cheated me.

Why: Cover "I thought": the man who was honest. The verb was needs a subject, so who.

Check yourself

"She is the girl ____ we believe will top." (who / whom)

who.

"She is the girl ____ we chose as captain." (who / whom)

whom. We chose her.

Likely next: "____ do you think will win?" (Who / Whom)

Who.

Chapter 06 · WEAK SPOT · Q31

6. Complete comparisons

In "as tall as, if not taller than", read each half alone with the end of the sentence. Both must be complete.

  • Half 1: He is as tall as his brother. Complete.
  • Half 2: He is taller than his brother. Complete.
✗ WrongHe is as tall, if not taller than, his brother. ("as tall his brother")
✓ RightHe is as tall as, if not taller than, his brother.

Same family: compare like with like. "The climate of Puri is milder than that of Bhubaneswar"; plural → those of.

Q31 · you chose: He is as tall, if not taller than, his brother.

Choose the grammatically correct sentence:

  • He is as tall as, if not taller than, his brother. ✓ right
  • He is as tall, if not taller than, his brother. ✗ your pick
  • He is as tall as, if not taller, his brother.
  • He is tall as, if not taller than, his brother.

Why: Your pick reads "as tall his brother" without the second as.

Check yourself

"The roads here are better than ____ of the city." (that / those)

those.

Likely next: "He is senior ____ me." (than / to)

to. Senior, junior, superior, prior take "to".

Chapter 07 · WEAK SPOT · Q22, Q25, Q26

7. before, but, seeing

A word's job in the sentence decides its part of speech 2021 Q17–22.

WordTestPart of speech
before / after / sincenothing followsAdverb: I have met him before.
before / after / sincea noun followsPreposition: before lunch.
before / after / sincesubject + verb followsConjunction: before they arrive.
butcan be replaced by "except"Preposition: All but one passed.
-ing wordit is the subject or objectGerund: Walking is healthy.
-ing / -ed wordit describes the subjectParticiple: Seeing the police, the thief ran.

Q22 · you chose: Preposition

Choose the correct part of speech for the word in brackets:

I am sure I have met this gentleman somewhere (before).

  • Adverb ✓ right
  • Preposition ✗ your pick
  • Conjunction
  • Adjective

Why: Nothing comes after before, so it is an adverb.

  • Preposition ✓ right

  • Participle ✓ right
  • Gerund
  • Infinitive
  • Verb

Chapter 08 · WEAK SPOT · Q34, Q36

8. scenery; a pair of shoes

Both misses had a plural word next to the verb that was not the subject. Cross out the "of …" part and find the real subject.

  • Scenery, furniture, luggage, machinery, information, advice, news, poetry are uncountable: singular verb.
  • A pair of shoes / trousers / scissors: the subject is pair, so singular.
✗ WrongThe scenery of the Koraput hills are charming.
✓ RightThe scenery of the Koraput hills is charming.
✓ RightThe pair of shoes that I bought last week is tight.
✓ RightThese shoes are tight. (no "pair": plural)

Q34 · you chose: the Koraput hills

Identify the incorrect segment:

[The scenery of] [the Koraput hills] [are charming] [during the monsoon.]

  • The scenery of
  • the Koraput hills ✗ your pick
  • are charming ✓ right
  • during the monsoon.

Why: Cross out "of the Koraput hills": The scenery … is.

Q36 · you chose: are

The pair of shoes that I bought at the fair last week ____ already too tight for me.

  • is ✓ right
  • are ✗ your pick
  • were
  • have been

Why: The subject is pair, which is singular.

Check yourself

"The furniture in these rooms ____ new." (is / are)

is.

"A pair of scissors ____ on the table." (is / are)

is.

"My spectacles ____ broken." (is / are)

are.

Chapter 09 · WEAK SPOT · Q18, Q32, Q63

9. resemble, dispense with, part with

Two traps: adding a preposition where none is needed, and picking the wrong fixed one.

No preposition: resemble, discuss, reach, enter, marry, attack, approach.

✗ WrongThe boy resembles with his father.
✓ RightThe boy resembles his father.
✗ WrongThey discussed about the plan.
✓ RightThey discussed the plan.
Fixed pairMeaning
dispense withdo without
part withgive away a thing
part fromleave a person
cope withmanage a difficulty
dispose ofget rid of
comply withobey

Q32 · you chose: The boy resembles with his father in every way.

Choose the grammatically correct sentence:

  • The boy resembles his father in every way. ✓ right
  • The boy resembles with his father in every way. ✗ your pick
  • The boy is resembling his father in every way.
  • The boy resembles to his father in every way.

Why: Resemble takes no preposition, and no -ing.

Q18 · you skipped it

As the chief guest was in a hurry, the organisers decided to dispense ____ the usual speeches and began the prize distribution at once.

  • with ✓ right
  • of
  • off
  • from

Why: Dispense with = do without.

Q63 · you chose: away

The rich merchant refused to part ____ even a single coin for the repair of the village temple.

  • with ✓ right
  • from
  • off
  • away ✗ your pick

Why: Part with a thing ("give away" misled you).

Check yourself

"The train reached ____ Puri at noon." (at / nothing)

nothing: reached Puri.

"Please dispose ____ the old files." (off / of)

of.

Likely next: "He married ____ his friend." (with / nothing)

10. Six words

Each time you picked a near trap: a word linked to the meaning, not the meaning. Say the meaning in your own words before reading the options.

Q43 · you chose: false

Choose the word closest in meaning to the word in brackets:

The court said that it would not waste its time on such (frivolous) complaints.

  • trivial ✓ right
  • serious
  • false ✗ your pick
  • angry

Why: Frivolous = not serious, silly. A complaint can be true and still frivolous.

Q44 · you skipped it

Choose the word most similar in meaning to AMELIORATE:

  • Improve ✓ right
  • Worsen
  • Measure
  • Accept

Why: Latin melior = better.

Q45 · you chose: (II) and (IV)

Select the pair of synonyms:

(I) Diligent (II) Careless (III) Industrious (IV) Idle

  • (I) and (III) ✓ right
  • (II) and (IV) ✗ your pick
  • (I) and (II)
  • (III) and (IV)

Why: Diligent = industrious = hardworking. Careless and idle are both faults, but they do not mean the same.

Q47 · you chose: Clarity

Choose the word most opposite in meaning to SUBVERSION:

  • Compliance ✓ right
  • Destabilisation
  • Clarity ✗ your pick
  • Sanity

Why: Subversion = undermining rules; opposite = obeying them. Destabilisation is the synonym trap.

Q51 · you chose: Anger

Choose the word most opposite in meaning to ZEAL:

  • Apathy ✓ right
  • Passion
  • Anger ✗ your pick
  • Courage

Why: Zeal = eager energy; opposite = no feeling. Passion is the synonym trap.

Q54 · you chose: eloquent

The speech was so ____ that the listeners, who had expected a long lecture, were pleased when the speaker finished in five minutes.

  • succinct ✓ right
  • verbose
  • eloquent ✗ your pick
  • hesitant

Why: The clue "five minutes" is about length. Succinct = short and clear.

10.1Two habits

  • Antonyms: the options usually hide a synonym. Cross it out, then pick its opposite 2021 Q46.
  • Word in a sentence: find the clue. In Q54 it was about length, so a word about fine speech (eloquent) could not fit.
