Reasoning and Mental Ability (Q1–50)

Q1Select the correct option that indicates the arrangement of the given words in the order in which they appear in a telephone directory: (I) Krishanmurty (II) Krishnamurthy (III) Krishnmurthi (IV) Krishanmurthy (V) Krishnamurti

(A) (IV), (I), (II), (V), (III) (B) (IV), (I), (II), (III), (V) (C) (IV), (V), (II), (III), (I) (D) (IV), (III), (V), (III), (II)

AnswerA — Compare letter by letter. "Krisha…" comes before "Krishn…". Between the two "Krisha" words, "Krishanmurth…" (h) comes before "Krishanmurty" (y), so IV, I. Of the "Krishn" words, "Krishna…" comes before "Krishnm…", so II and V come before III. II (Krishnamurth…) comes before V (Krishnamurti) because h comes before i. Order: IV, I, II, V, III.

Q2In a certain code language, 'VIRTUE' is coded as '201' and 'TRAGEDY' is coded as '218'. How will 'PROFANE' be coded in that language?

(A) 570 (B) 342 (C) 432 (D) 456

AnswerB — Number each letter from the back (Z = 1, A = 26) and add them. Multiply by the number of vowels. VIRTUE: 5 + 18 + 9 + 7 + 6 + 22 = 67, vowels I, U, E = 3, so 67 × 3 = 201. TRAGEDY: 109 × 2 (vowels A, E) = 218. PROFANE: 11 + 9 + 12 + 21 + 26 + 13 + 22 = 114, vowels O, A, E = 3, so 114 × 3 = 342.

Directions (Q. Nos. 3 to 5): One hundred and twenty five cubes of the same size are arranged in the form of a cube on a table. Then a column of five cubes is removed from each of the four corners. All the exposed faces of the rest of the solid (except the face touching the table) are coloured red. Now answer these questions:

Q3How many small cubes are there in the solid after the removal of the columns?

(A) 120 (B) 110 (C) 105 (D) 100

AnswerC — 125 − (4 × 5) = 105.

Q4How many cubes do not have any coloured face?

(A) 12 (B) 24 (C) 36 (D) 48

AnswerC — The base is a 5 × 5 square with four corners cut away. A cube has no colour only if it is not in the top layer and all four of its sides touch other cubes. That means the middle 3 × 3 block, in the lower 4 layers: 9 × 4 = 36.

Q5How many cubes have only one red face?

(A) 40 (B) 25 (C) 20 (D) 15

AnswerB — Top layer, middle 3 × 3 block: 9 cubes (top face only). Lower 4 layers, the 4 middle cubes of each outer edge (one side face only): 4 × 4 = 16. Total 25.

Q6David gets on the elevator at the 11th floor of a building and rides up at the rate of 57 floors per minute. At the same time, Albert gets on an elevator at the 51st floor of the same building and rides down at the rate of 63 floors per minute. If they continue travelling at these rates, then at which floor will their paths cross?

(A) 19 (B) 28 (C) 30 (D) 37

AnswerC — The gap is 40 floors. They close it at 57 + 63 = 120 floors a minute, so they meet after 1/3 minute. David has gone 57 ÷ 3 = 19 floors up: 11 + 19 = 30.

Q7An egg vendor calls on his first customer and sells half his eggs and half an egg. To the second customer, he sells half of what he has left with and half an egg, and to the third customer, he sells half of what he was then left with and half an egg. However, he did not break any egg. If in the end, the vendor was left with three eggs. How many eggs did he have initially?

(A) 26 (B) 31 (C) 39 (D) None of these

AnswerB — Work backwards. Before the third sale: (3 + ½) × 2 = 7. Before the second: (7 + ½) × 2 = 15. Before the first: (15 + ½) × 2 = 31.

Q10Select the option in which the numbers are related in the same way as are the numbers of the following set, (24, 10, 392):

(A) (29, 18, 242) (B) (27, 15, 480) (C) (26, 12, 369) (D) (21, 18, 234)

AnswerA — The rule is 2 × (first − second)². (24 − 10) = 14, and 2 × 14² = 392. Check (A): 29 − 18 = 11, and 2 × 11² = 242.

Q11There are deer and peacocks in a zoo. By counting heads they are 80. The number of their legs is 200. How many peacocks are there?

(A) 20 (B) 30 (C) 50 (D) 60

AnswerD — If all 80 were peacocks there would be 160 legs. We have 40 extra legs. Each deer adds 2 extra legs, so 20 deer and 60 peacocks.

Q12What day of the week was 31st January, 2007?

(A) Tuesday (B) Monday (C) Thursday (D) Wednesday

AnswerD — 1 January 2007 was a Monday. 31 January is 30 days later. 30 ÷ 7 leaves 2, so Monday + 2 = Wednesday.

Q13Arrange the following words in the order in which they appear in an English dictionary: (I) Meticulous (II) Metric (III) Method (IV) Mettle (V) Meter

(A) (V), (III), (I), (IV), (II) (B) (V), (III), (I), (II), (IV) (C) (III), (IV), (V), (I), (II) (D) (V), (I), (III), (II), (IV)

AnswerB — All start with "Met". The next letters are: Meter (e), Method (h), Meticulous (i), Metric (r), Mettle (t). Order: V, III, I, II, IV.

Q14Rasik walked 20m towards north. Then he turned right and walks 30m. Then he turns right and walks 35m. Then he turns left and walks 15m. Finally he turns left and walks 15m. In which direction and how many metres is he from the starting position?

(A) 15m West (B) 30m East (C) 30m West (D) 45m East

AnswerD — North 20, East 30, South 35, East 15, North 15. Up-and-down: 20 − 35 + 15 = 0. East: 30 + 15 = 45. So 45 m East.

Q15How is 'sure' written in a code language? I. 'he is sure' written as 'ja ha ma' in that code language. II. 'is she sure' written as 'ka ja ma' in that code language.

(A) Ja (B) Ja or ma (C) Ma (D) Ha

AnswerB — "is" and "sure" are in both sentences, and "ja" and "ma" are in both codes. So "sure" is either "ja" or "ma" (we cannot tell which).

Q16Pointing to a woman, Nirmal said, "She is the daughter of my wife's grandmother's only child". How is the woman related to Nirmal if she is not the wife of Nirmal?

(A) Wife (B) Sister-in-law (C) Sister (D) None of these

AnswerB — Wife's grandmother's only child is the wife's mother or father. That person's daughter is the wife or the wife's sister. She is not the wife, so she is the wife's sister: Nirmal's sister-in-law.

Q17If ZIP = 198 and ZAP = 246, then how will you code VIP?

(A) 174 (B) 222 (C) 888 (D) 990

AnswerB — Number the letters from the back (Z = 1). Add them, add the number of letters, multiply by 6. ZIP: 1 + 18 + 11 = 30, plus 3 = 33, × 6 = 198. ZAP: 1 + 26 + 11 = 38, plus 3 = 41, × 6 = 246. VIP: 5 + 18 + 11 = 34, plus 3 = 37, × 6 = 222.

(A) Cousins (B) Husband-Wife (C) Father-Daughter (D) Uncle-Niece

Q21In a certain code, MOTHER is written as ONHURF. How will ANSWER be written in that code?

(A) NBXSSE (B) NBWRRF (C) MAVSPE (D) NBWTRF

AnswerD — Swap each pair of letters, then add 0 and +1 in turn. MOTHER → OMHTRE → O, N, H, U, R, F. ANSWER → NAWSRE → N, B, W, T, R, F.

Q22Find the odd one out: (A) Platform (B) Dock (C) Bus-stand (D) Park

AnswerD — Platform, dock and bus-stand are places where passengers or goods are loaded and vehicles stop. A park is not. Check (judgement question).

Q23Find the odd one out: (A) Lion (B) Tiger (C) Fox (D) Deer

AnswerD — Lion, tiger and fox eat meat. The deer eats only plants.

Q24A doctor said to his compounder "I go to see the patients at their residence after every 3 hours 30 minutes. I have already gone to the patient 1 hour 20 minutes ago and next time I shall go at 1:40 P.M." At what time this information was given to the compounder by the doctor?

(A) 11:30 A.M. (B) 11:20 A.M. (C) 10:10 A.M. (D) None of these

AnswerA — Last visit was 3 h 30 min before 1:40 P.M., at 10:10 A.M. He said it 1 h 20 min after that: 11:30 A.M.

Q25If in the word SEPTUAGENARIAN first three and then next three letters are written in reverse order and the rest of the letters are written as they appear in English alphabet, the positions of how many letters get changed in the new arrangement?

(A) Nil (B) 2 (C) 10 (D) 12

AnswerD — The word has 14 letters. SEP TUA → PES AUT. The rest, GENARIAN, in alphabet order is AAEGINNR. First six letters: S E P T U A → P E S A U T. E and U stay, 4 change. Last eight: G E N A R I A N → A A E G I N N R. All 8 change. Total 12.

Q28Seven years from now, Anamika will be as old as Malini was 4 years ago. Srinidhi was born 2 years ago. The average age of Anamika, Malini and Srinidhi 10 years from now will be 33 years. What is the present age of Anamika?

(A) 30 years (B) 31 years (C) 29 years (D) 28 years

AnswerD — Let Anamika be A. Then Malini = A + 11, and Srinidhi = 2. Ten years later the ages total 33 × 3 = 99. So (A + 10) + (A + 21) + 12 = 99, 2A + 43 = 99, A = 28.

Q29Nurture : Neglect :: Denigrate : ?

(A) Reveal (B) Extol (C) Recognise (D) Calumniate

AnswerB — Nurture and neglect are opposites. Denigrate means to run down. Its opposite is extol (praise highly). Calumniate means the same as denigrate.

Q34In a certain code language, "GOAT" is written as "45" and "COAT" is written as "41". How is "BOAT" written in that code language?

(A) 40 (B) 41 (C) 42 (D) 43

AnswerA — Add the letter numbers and add 2. GOAT: 7 + 15 + 1 + 20 = 43, plus 2 = 45. COAT: 39 + 2 = 41. BOAT: 2 + 15 + 1 + 20 = 38, plus 2 = 40.

Q35In a certain code language, TUTORIAL is written as DODNGLCF and DANCE is written as YCJMZ, how is EDUCATION written in that code?

(A) ZYMODCLNJ (B) ZYOMCDLNJ (C) ZYOMDCLNJ (D) ZYOTNLCMD

AnswerB — Each letter has one fixed code. From the two words: T = D, U = O, O = N, R = G, I = L, A = C, L = F, D = Y, N = J, C = M, E = Z. EDUCATION: E Z, D Y, U O, C M, A C, T D, I L, O N, N J = ZYOMCDLNJ.

Q36The door of Aditya's house faces the East. From the back side of his house, he walks straight 50 meters, then turns to the right and walks 50 meters again. Finally, he turns towards left and stops after walking 25 meters. Now, Aditya is in which direction from the starting point?

(A) South-East (B) North-East (C) South-West (D) North-West

AnswerD — The back of the house is West. West 50, then right turn to North 50, then left turn to West 25. He is 75 m west and 50 m north: North-West.

Q37A man is facing towards West and turns through 45° clock-wise, again 180° clock-wise and then turns through 270° anti clock-wise. In which direction is he facing now?

(A) West (B) North-West (C) North (D) South-West

AnswerD — West + 45° clockwise = North-West. + 180° = South-East. 270° anticlockwise is the same as 90° clockwise, giving South-West.

Mathematics (Q51–100)

Q511/(1·3) + 1/(3·5) + 1/(5·7) + 1/(7·9) = ?

(A) 5/11 (B) 6/11 (C) 9/4 (D) 4/9

AnswerD — Each term is ½ × (1/a − 1/b), so the sum is ½ × (1 − 1/9) = ½ × 8/9 = 4/9.

Q52Determine the smallest 3-digit number which is exactly divisible by 6 and 12?

(A) 96 (B) 84 (C) 108 (D) 120

AnswerC — The LCM of 6 and 12 is 12. The smallest 3-digit multiple of 12 is 108 (12 × 9).

Q53What is the HCF of 1/5, 2/7 and 3/11?

(A) 1/385 (B) 6 (C) 1/35 (D) 5/77

AnswerA — HCF of fractions = HCF of the tops ÷ LCM of the bottoms = 1 ÷ 385.

Q5911 oranges are bought for Rs. 10 and 10 oranges for Rs. 11. What is the gain in percentage?

(A) 11% (B) 21% (C) 25% (D) 28%

AnswerB — The question means he buys at 11 for Rs. 10 and sells at 10 for Rs. 11. Cost of one: 10/11. Sale price of one: 11/10. Gain = (11/10) ÷ (10/11) − 1 = 121/100 − 1 = 21%.

Q60The difference between Compound Interest and Simple Interest for 2 years at 5% per annum is Rs. 2.50. Find the sum:

(A) 500 (B) 1500 (C) 1000 (D) None of these

AnswerC — For 2 years, CI − SI = P × (r/100)² = P × 0.0025. So P = 2.50 ÷ 0.0025 = Rs. 1000.

Q61A can finish a work in 18 days and B can do the same work in 15 days. B worked for 10 days and left the job. In how many days will 10 women complete it?

(A) 5 (B) 5⅓ (C) 6 (D) 8

AnswerC — Check — The "10 women" is a misprint; the sums only work for "A alone". B does 10/15 = 2/3 of the work. 1/3 is left. A does it in 18 ÷ 3 = 6 days.

Q62The ratio between the speeds of two trains is 7:8. If the second train runs 400 kms in 4 hours, then the speed of first train is:

(A) 70 km/h (B) 75 km/h (C) 84 km/h (D) 87.5 km/h

AnswerD — Second train: 400 ÷ 4 = 100 km/h. First: 100 × 7/8 = 87.5 km/h.

Q64If α and β are the roots of quadratic equation such that α + β = 12 and α − β = 4, then the equation is:

(A) x² − 12x + 32 = 0 (B) x² − 12x − 32 = 0 (C) x² + 12x + 32 = 0 (D) x² + 12x − 32 = 0

AnswerA — α = 8, β = 4. Sum 12, product 32, so x² − 12x + 32 = 0.

Q65If for p ≠ 1, p^(5x+3) = 1 then x = ?

(A) −2/5 (B) 3/5 (C) −3/5 (D) 2/5

AnswerC — A number other than 1 (or 0, −1) to a power gives 1 only if the power is 0. 5x + 3 = 0, so x = −3/5.

Q66If n is a natural number, then (6n² + 6n) is always divisible by:

(A) 6 only (B) 6 and 12 only (C) 12 only (D) 18 only

AnswerB — 6n² + 6n = 6n(n + 1). n(n + 1) is always even, so the whole thing is a multiple of 12, and so also of 6. It is not always a multiple of 18 (n = 1 gives 12).

Q67If x/5 = y/9 then (x + 5) : (y + 9) = ?

(A) 3:5 (B) 13:8 (C) 5:9 (D) 9:5

AnswerC — Put x = 5k and y = 9k. Then (5k + 5) : (9k + 9) = 5(k + 1) : 9(k + 1) = 5 : 9.

Q68The fourth proportional to 5, 8, 15 is:

(A) 18 (B) 21 (C) 19 (D) 24

AnswerD — 5 : 8 = 15 : x, so x = 8 × 15 ÷ 5 = 24.

Q69If the determinant |x+y, y; 3−x, 3| = |2, −1; 0, 3|, then the value of x and y is:

(A) 3, −1 (B) −3, −1 (C) −3, 1 (D) 3, 1

AnswerA — Left: 3(x + y) − y(3 − x) = 3x + xy. Right: 6. So x(3 + y) = 6. Only x = 3, y = −1 gives 3 × 2 = 6.

Q73If a pair of linear equations is given by a₁x + b₁y + c₁ = 0 and a₂x + b₂y + c₂ = 0 where a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ then:
