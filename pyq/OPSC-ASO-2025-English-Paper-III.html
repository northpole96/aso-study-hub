What changed in 2025: there were no idioms, no synonym/antonym lists, no voice, no narration and no analogies. Instead, almost two-thirds of the paper was fill-in-the-blanks. Jumbled sentences grew to 20 questions, and comprehension doubled to two passages. The second passage was about Odisha's hockey investment.

Fill in the blanks (Q1–64)

Directions: Fill in the blanks in the following sentences by selecting the most appropriate option from the choices given below each.

Q1In retrospect, I wish I ____ more time for this project.

(A) have (B) had (C) will have (D) having

AnswerB — "Wish" about the past needs a past form. ("Had had" would be ideal, but of these only "had" works.)

Q2His explanation was so ____ that everyone understood the difficult concept.

(A) complicated (B) clear (C) confusing (D) vague

AnswerB

Q3His ____ nature always brings laughter to the group.

(A) indifferent (B) humorous (C) gloomy (D) serious

AnswerB

Q4Although Mala is the oldest child in her group, she is the ____ of all.

(A) innocent (B) more innocent (C) most innocent (D) innocently

AnswerC — "of all" needs the superlative.

Q5His ____ outlook on life is refreshing, unlike the pessimism of his brothers.

(A) dark (B) positive (C) negative (D) grim

AnswerB

Q6This will prevent companies ____ hazardous waste improperly.

(A) disposed (B) to dispose (C) from disposing (D) dispose

AnswerC — prevent someone from doing.

Q7We meet ____, in June and in December, to review the progress.

(A) annually (B) biannually (C) weekly (D) monthly

AnswerB — Twice a year.

Q8____ did the child touch the tree than it broke out into blossoms.

(A) No sooner (B) Only (C) Hardly (D) Narrowly

AnswerA — "No sooner … than". ("Hardly" goes with "when".)

Q9The team, as well as their coach, ____ optimistic about the season ahead.

(A) are (B) is (C) were (D) be

AnswerB — The verb agrees with the first subject; "as well as" does not make it plural.

Q10The train ____ before she started from home.

(A) left (B) has left (C) had left (D) will leave

AnswerC — Past perfect for the earlier action.

Q11____ does one encounter such six-hitting feat by an Indian batsman.

(A) Infrequent (B) Hard (C) Few (D) Hardly

AnswerD — A negative adverb at the start causes inversion ("Hardly does one…").

Q12Very few students in the class have performed ____ well.

(A) vary (B) reasonably (C) perfect (D) timely

AnswerB — Only an adverb can modify "well".

Q13Had she known about the meeting, she ____ there on time.

(A) would be (B) would have been (C) will be (D) is

AnswerB — Third conditional.

Q14His expertise lies ____ the development of renewable energy technologies.

(A) on (B) in (C) for (D) to

AnswerB — Expertise lies in.

Q15The ____ findings of the research were welcomed by the academic community.

(A) mundane (B) insightful (C) superficial (D) abstract

AnswerB

Q16His actions were seen as ____, while most expected him to be more considerate.

(A) compassionate (B) polite (C) selfish (D) generous

AnswerC

Q17The house looked ____ as it had an unwelcoming and unattractive air about it.

(A) bleak (B) pleasant (C) engaging (D) enticing

AnswerA

Q18The committee finally ____ at a decision after hours of debate.

(A) arrived (B) came (C) went (D) drew

AnswerA — "arrive at a decision". ("Came to a decision" would need "to".)

Q19The scientists scrutinised the data ____ to ensure accuracy.

(A) thorough (B) through (C) meticulously (D) hardly

AnswerC

Q20A number of factors ____ influenced the outcome of the project.

(A) was (B) have (C) has (D) is

AnswerB — "A number of" = many, so plural. ("The number of" is singular.)

Q21____ did she articulate her views that the audience sat in stunned silence.

(A) So compellingly (B) Such compelling (C) How compellingly (D) Thus compelling

AnswerA — "So + adverb … that", with inversion.

Q22The solution ____ remains controversial, despite earnest efforts.

(A) still (B) yet (C) may (D) not with standing

AnswerA

Q23She ____ in New York for five years now.

(A) living (B) lives (C) has lived (D) lived

AnswerC — "for five years now" needs the present perfect.

Q24I will be visiting ____ Amazon rainforest next year.

(A) a (B) an (C) the (D) no article

AnswerC — A unique, named natural feature takes "the".

Q25If it rains tomorrow, I ____ my umbrella.

(A) will bring (B) brings (C) was bringing (D) be bringing

AnswerA — First conditional.

Q26He has a ____ attitude towards his colleagues, making the workplace enjoyable.

(A) negative (B) critical (C) cheerful (D) indifferent

AnswerC

Q27They ____ each other for over a decade when they finally met.

(A) have known (B) had known (C) know (D) were knowing

AnswerB

Q28The weather was ____ yesterday, but today it feels quite chilly.

(A) warm (B) cool (C) cold (D) rainy

AnswerA — "But" signals a contrast with chilly.

Q29She has a ____ interest in classical literature and often discusses it passionately.

(A) profound (B) shallow (C) casual (D) limited

AnswerA

Q30The author's style is considered quite ____, often leaving readers enthralled.

(A) challenging (B) engaging (C) dull (D) uninteresting

AnswerB

Q31She has ____ unique perspective on the issue.

(A) a (B) an (C) the (D) none of the above

AnswerA — "Unique" starts with a "yoo" sound, so "a".

Q32The hypothesis was ____ untenable, collapsing under peer review.

(A) utterly (B) uttering (C) utter (D) uttered

AnswerA — An adverb modifies the adjective "untenable".

Q33The initial experiment failed. ____, the team learned valuable lessons.

(A) However (B) Sparingly (C) Meticulously (D) Since

AnswerA

Q34The witness ____ having been near the scene that night.

(A) relied (B) admitted (C) expected (D) failed

AnswerB — "admit + -ing".

Q35A group of students ____ planning to go to the beach now.

(A) be (B) were (C) are (D) is

AnswerD — "A group" is singular. (Some keys accept "are". Check)

Q36He deeply ____ for not visiting his grandfather before he passed away. [printed as "regretted … for"]

(A) avoided (B) regretted (C) denied (D) finished

AnswerB

Q37She usually ____ breakfast at 7 AM.

(A) eat (B) eats (C) eating (D) has eaten

AnswerB — A habit takes the simple present.

Q38If I ____ you, I would have seized that opportunity immediately.

(A) am (B) were (C) was (D) have been

AnswerB — "If I were you" is fixed.

Q39Neither of the candidates ____ very experienced in this field.

(A) is (B) are (C) be (D) were

AnswerA — "Neither of" takes a singular verb.

Q40____ had such data been accessible to researchers outside elite institutions.

(A) Never before (B) Before never (C) Heretofore (D) Hitherto

AnswerA — Negative opening + inversion.

Q41The data was analysed ____, yielding insights that revolutionized the field.

(A) exhaustively (B) exhaustive (C) exhaustion (D) in exhaust

AnswerA

Q42The study focuses ____ the relationship between diet and mental health.

(A) in (B) at (C) on (D) with

AnswerC

Q43She was feeling quite ____ after working late into the night.

(A) content (B) exhausted (C) energetic (D) happy

AnswerB

Q44The youngest runner finished the race ____, surprising all the coaches.

(A) quick (B) quicker (C) quickly (D) quickness

AnswerC

Q45After the bitter quarrel, he felt a sense of ____ for losing temper.

(A) lethargy (B) remorse (C) ennui (D) apathy

AnswerB — Remorse = regret for a wrong done. Ennui = boredom.

Q46His performance was considered ____ compared to the others, who excelled.

(A) brilliant (B) exceptional (C) impressive (D) mediocre

AnswerD

Q47Despite the harsh weather, the team remained ____ in their effort to complete the project.

(A) fearful (B) indifferent (C) pessimistic (D) optimistic

AnswerD

Q48She had asked them ____ interrupt her during the presentation.

(A) not (B) don't (C) not to (D) will

AnswerC — "ask someone not to do".

Q49He spoke ____, avoiding any terminology that might escalate tensions.

(A) measured (B) measuredly (C) in a measuring way (D) with measure

AnswerB — The only adverb that means "carefully and calmly". Check (an unusual word; "in a measured way" would be more natural, but it is not offered)

Q50The team's ____ was evident in the way they played together and won.

(A) difference (B) unity (C) discord (D) cacophony

AnswerB

Q51We are unhappy because the results of the experiment are ____ to our expectations.

(A) contrary (B) consistent (C) supportive (D) expected

AnswerA

Q52____, the data strongly contradicts the initial hypothesis.

(A) No sooner does (B) Summary (C) In as much as (D) Significantly

AnswerD

Q53The collection of rare manuscripts ____ housed in the climate-controlled archive.

(A) are (B) were (C) have been (D) is

AnswerD — The subject is "collection" (singular).

Q54The intricate design ____ meticulous attention during manufacturing.

(A) calls for (B) comes to (C) gets over (D) puts off

AnswerA — "call for" = need.

Q55Each of the buildings ____ unique design.

(A) have (B) has (C) are (D) were

AnswerB — "Each" is singular.

Q56The mineral deposits lie ____, beyond the reach of conventional mining.

(A) subterranean (B) subterraneously (C) extraterrestrial (D) insubordinately

AnswerA — "Subterranean" = underground. After "lie" (a state), the adjective is used.

Q57It was ____ honour to meet the author.

(A) a (B) an (C) the (D) some

AnswerB — The h in "honour" is silent.

Q58The concert was so ____ that many people were moved to tears.

(A) chaotic (B) touching (C) dull (D) loud

AnswerB

Q59The novel is known for its ____ style, which contrasts sharply with the author's earlier complex work.

(A) simple (B) happy (C) intricate (D) shallow

AnswerA

Q60She ____ her initial enthusiasm, giving her team a positive outlook.

(A) gave up (B) held on to (C) put up with (D) got over

AnswerB

Q61Her final exam results were ____ surprising, even to her parents.

(A) meticulously (B) fast (C) slowly (D) hardly

AnswerD — It is the only adverb of degree on offer. The sentence is oddly made, because "even to her parents" suggests "surprisingly". Check

Q62The pair of shoes ____ on sale at the store.

(A) is (B) are (C) be (D) were

AnswerA — "The pair" is singular.

Q63They ____ the Asia Cup trophy in 1993.

(A) win (B) has won (C) won (D) winning

AnswerC — A past date takes the simple past.

Q64The researchers conducted an experiment ____ the effects of climate change.

(A) in (B) on (C) under (D) for

AnswerB

Spot the incorrect segment (Q65–70)

Directions: Identify the incorrect segment in the sentences.

Q65Neither the manager [nor] his assistant [were] aware of the new regulations, and this [caused] significant confusion [among] the staff.

(A) nor (B) were (C) caused (D) among

AnswerB — With "neither … nor", the verb agrees with the nearer subject ("assistant"), so "was".

Q66[If] I had [been] rich in my youth, I [will] have [bought] a car.

(A) If (B) been (C) will (D) bought

AnswerC — It should be "would have bought".

Q67Everyone [feel] a great [sense of] elation [when] one [gets] a dream job.

(A) feel (B) sense of (C) when (D) gets

AnswerA — "Everyone feels".

Q68Immigration rules [has become] so stringent [that] visa verification [happens] at multiple levels before a passenger [is given] entry.

(A) has become (B) that (C) happens (D) is given

AnswerA — "Rules have become".

Q69The committee, which [has reached] a decision, [are] now preparing [their] official report [for publication].

(A) which (B) has reached (C) are (D) their

AnswerC — The sentence already treats "committee" as singular ("has reached"), so "is". (D "their" would then also need to be "its", but C is the first clear error.)

Q70Not only [does] the rain ruin the picnic, [but also] the wind made [it impossible] to set up the tent [in the slope].

(A) does (B) but also (C) it impossible (D) in the slope

AnswerA — The other clause is in the past ("made"), so "did". Check: "in the slope" should also be "on the slope", so D is defensible too.

Rearrange the parts into a sentence (Q71–84)

Directions: Rearrange the four parts marked P, Q, R, S to make a correct sentence.

Q71(P) in her writing (Q) significant improvements (R) over the years (S) she has made

(A) SQPR (B) QSPR (C) SRPQ (D) RQSP

AnswerA — She has made significant improvements in her writing over the years.

Q72(P) has been (Q) reading romantic novels (R) and going to sleep (S) her habit

(A) SPRQ (B) RSPQ (C) QRPS (D) PRSQ

Answernone exactly. — The correct order is SPQR (Her habit has been reading romantic novels and going to sleep), which is not offered. A (SPRQ) is closest and was probably intended. Check

Q73(P) my sister (Q) likes to bake (R) on weekends (S) cookies

(A) PQSR (B) PRQS (C) PRSQ (D) RPSQ

AnswerA — My sister likes to bake cookies on weekends.

Q74(P) the team (Q) won the championship (R) when they (S) was ecstatic

(A) PSRQ (B) RPSQ (C) SQRP (D) SPQR

AnswerA — The team was ecstatic when they won the championship.

Q75(P) she (Q) enjoys hiking (R) with her friends (S) and partying

(A) PSQR (B) PQSR (C) QPRS (D) PRSQ

AnswerB — She enjoys hiking and partying with her friends.

Q76(P) last semester (Q) I took (R) a course (S) on environmental science

(A) QPSR (B) RPSQ (C) PQRS (D) PRSQ

AnswerC — Last semester I took a course on environmental science.

Q77(P) modern technology (Q) the complexities of (R) understands (S) not everyone

(A) SRQP (B) PRSQ (C) SQPR (D) RPQS

AnswerA — Not everyone understands the complexities of modern technology.

Q78(P) their dinner (Q) went to the park (R) after finishing (S) her friends

(A) RSQP (B) ASPR [sic] (C) RPSQ (D) PQRS

AnswerC — After finishing their dinner, her friends went to the park.

Q79(P) after five years of research (Q) by her (R) was discovered (S) the cure

(A) SPQR (B) PQRS (C) QPRS (D) SRQP

AnswerD — The cure was discovered by her after five years of research.

Q80(P) motivated the team (Q) to enhance their performance (R) by their coach (S) the talk

(A) PRQS (B) SRPQ (C) PQRS (D) SQRP

AnswerB — The talk by their coach motivated the team to enhance their performance.

Q81(P) a vivid description (Q) painted (R) the author (S) of the scene

(A) RSQP (B) RQPS (C) PRSQ (D) RPSQ

AnswerB — The author painted a vivid description of the scene.

Q82(P) at the conference (Q) experts will discuss (R) the recent findings (S) on climate change

(A) QRSP (B) QSPR (C) QPSR (D) RPQS

AnswerA — Experts will discuss the recent findings on climate change at the conference.

Q83(P) to travel abroad (Q) he decided (R) for the treatment (S) of his son

(A) PSQR (B) RPQS (C) QPRS (D) PRSQ

AnswerC — He decided to travel abroad for the treatment of his son.

Q84(P) a new laptop (Q) for his school (R) the teacher bought (S) yesterday

(A) RSQP (B) RPQS (C) PSRQ (D) QPRS

AnswerB — The teacher bought a new laptop for his school yesterday.

Rearrange the sentences into a paragraph (Q85–90)

Q85(P) However, the new policy is designed to benefit everyone in the long run. (Q) It is aimed to increase efficiency in the office. (R) Recently, the company announced a new incentive policy. (S) Initially, the clerks are likely to face a few challenges.

(A) RPQS (B) RQSP (C) RPSQ (D) SRPQ

AnswerB — Announced → aim → early problems → "However" long-run benefit.

Q86(P) However, critics argue it stifles artistic freedom and innovation. (Q) The film industry relies heavily on established franchises and sequels. (R) This strategy minimizes financial risk for studios. (S) Audiences often show strong loyalty to familiar characters and stories.

(A) PRQS (B) SQPR (C) RPSQ (D) QSRP

AnswerD — Fact → why audiences like it → why studios like it → "However" criticism.

Q87(P) The strategy was promising and showed significant results. (Q) So, the management decided to expand the trial to more regions. (R) A new marketing strategy was piloted in select regions. (S) Customer feedback during the pilot was overwhelmingly positive.

(A) QRPS (B) PSQR (C) RPSQ (D) SRQP

AnswerC — Pilot → results → feedback → "So" expand.

Q88(P) This influx strained local infrastructure and resources. (Q) The scenic valley became a major tourist destination. (R) Consequently, authorities implemented visitor quotas. (S) Its popularity surged after a viral social media campaign.

(A) PRQS (B) SQRP (C) RPSQ (D) QSPR

AnswerD — Valley → viral fame → influx strained resources → "Consequently" quotas.

Q89(P) This lack of evidence led to the case being dismissed. (Q) The investigation uncovered several inconsistencies in the testimony. (R) Some people raised serious allegations against the official. (S) Forensic analysis also failed to corroborate the claims.

(A) RQSP (B) QRPS (C) PSRQ (D) SRQP

AnswerA — Allegations → investigation → forensics "also" failed → "This lack of evidence" dismissed.

Q90(P) This decentralization enhances security and transparency. (Q) Blockchain technology operates on a distributed ledger system. (R) Each participant in the network holds a copy of the ledger. (S) Transactions are recorded simultaneously across multiple nodes.

(A) SPQR (B) RSQP (C) QRSP (D) PQRS

AnswerC — Define → each holds a copy → recorded on all nodes → "This decentralization" benefit.

Passage 1 (Q91–95)

Globalization has reshaped education by enabling access to a vast array of resources and facilitating communication among students worldwide. Students can now interact with peers and educators across the globe, learning from diverse perspectives. This connection fosters a greater understanding of global issues and cultural differences. Additionally, globalization promotes the adoption of international curriculums, such as the International Baccalaureate (IB), which are designed to cultivate skills needed in a global workforce. However, this shift can also lead to a neglect of local educational values and content, potentially diluting cultural identities. The increasing reliance on technology further complicates this landscape, as students must navigate online learning environments while developing critical thinking skills. Overall, while globalization enhances educational opportunities and cross-cultural exchanges, it also poses challenges that stakeholders must address to preserve local cultures and maintain educational integrity.

Q91What international curriculum is mentioned as being promoted by globalization?

(A) National Core Curriculum (B) Advanced Placement (C) International Baccalaureate (D) Traditional Local Curriculum

AnswerC

Q92What challenge does globalization pose to local education according to the passage?

(A) Enhanced technological resources (B) An emphasis on standardized tests (C) Increased cultural understanding (D) Neglect of local educational values

AnswerD

Q93What critical skill must students develop while navigating online learning environments?

(A) Critical thinking skills (B) Memorization (C) Basic computer knowledge (D) Time management

AnswerA

Q94The overall effect the passage suggests globalization has on education is that it:

(A) diminishes local identities (B) restricts educational opportunities (C) enhances educational opportunities and cultural exchanges (D) discourages international collaboration

AnswerC

Q95Globalization affected student interactions in education by:

(A) limiting communication (B) enabling access to resources (C) creating barriers to understanding (D) discouraging cultural exchanges

Passage 2 (Q96–100)

The State of Odisha has emerged as a pivotal force in Indian sports, primarily through its transformative investments in hockey. Hosting the 2018 Men's Hockey World Cup in Bhubaneswar and Rourkela—the latter featuring the world's largest hockey stadium—signalled its infrastructural ambition. The state government's corporate patronage, notably via PSUs like Odisha Mining Corporation, funds elite athlete development, including Olympic medallists in hockey and athletics. Beyond infrastructure, Odisha pioneered grassroots talent identification in tribal regions, focusing on hockey, rugby, and weightlifting. Its holistic development model integrates sports science, education, and international coaching partnerships. However, critics note disproportionate hockey funding overshadows emerging disciplines like gymnastics. Odisha's sustained commitment positions it as an indispensable catalyst for India's sporting ascendancy.
