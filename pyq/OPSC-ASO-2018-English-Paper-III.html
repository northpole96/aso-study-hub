Questions

Q1"May you live happily and long!" In very formal English, what does this express?

(A) Desire (B) Approval (C) Happiness (D) Wish

AnswerD — "May you…" is the formal form for a wish or blessing.

Q2Which of the following is more polite as a request?

(A) Will you lend me your scooter? (B) Would you lend me your scooter, please? (C) Can you lend me your scooter, please? (D) Shall I take your scooter on loan?

AnswerB — "Would … please" is the most polite.

Q3What does the following sentence really mean? He has been sleeping for five hours.

(A) He is sleeping on for hours. (B) He has lost sense of time. (C) And he is still sleeping. (D) And he will not wake up now.

AnswerC — The present perfect continuous means the action is still going on.

Q4"____ do you wish to see?" "____ wishes to see you?" (Fill in the blanks)

(A) Whom, Who (B) Who, Who (C) Whose, Who (D) Who, Whom

AnswerA — "Whom" is the object of "see". "Who" is the subject of "wishes".

Q5Only graduates are eligible ____ this post. You must apply ____ the Registrar for filing your application. The Registrar's office is adjacent ____ the Commissioner's. (Fill in the blanks)

(A) to, for, with (B) in, to, along (C) for, with, to (D) for, to, to

AnswerD — eligible for, apply to a person, adjacent to.

Q6In the sentence, The girls sang songs while the boys played hockey, "while" means:

(A) At some other time (B) At the same time (C) At different venues (D) At the same venue

AnswerB

Q7Determine the part of speech of "fast" in the following sentences: I kept the fast for a month. I cannot fast anymore. I ran fast. Still I could not catch the fast train to Bandra.

(A) Verb, adverb, adjective, noun (B) Noun, verb, adjective, adverb (C) Verb, adjective, noun, adverb (D) Noun, verb, adverb, adjective

AnswerD

Q8(Active) Someone has picked my pocket. In passive voice, this will read:

(A) My pocket was picked. (B) Who has picked my pocket? (C) Someone's pocket has been picked. (D) My pocket has been picked.

AnswerD — Keep the present perfect tense and drop "by someone".

Q9I have not met my brother lately. A phrase closest to "lately" would be:

(A) in the recent years (B) late in the year (C) later than last month (D) in the recent past

AnswerD

Q10Spot the incorrect sentence below:

(A) Don't talk so loud. (B) I can bowl fast. (C) She is naughtier. (D) You can only guess.

AnswerC — A comparative needs something to compare with ("naughtier than her sister").

Q11What does "ought to" in the following sentence suggest? We ought to love our neighbours.

(A) Compulsion and obligation (B) Moral obligation or desirability (C) Conscientious regard for the right (D) Religious commitment to good life

AnswerB

Q12Combine the following simple sentences into one complex sentence: He will be late. That is certain.

(A) That will be late for him certainly. (B) Certainly he will be late. (C) It will be certain that is late for him. (D) It is certain that he will be late.

AnswerD — Option B is a simple sentence, not a complex one.

Q13We derive nouns from some verbs. For example, from grieve we derive grief, from speak we derive speech, etc. Likewise, from weave we derive ____.

(A) weaving (B) woven (C) web (D) warp

AnswerC — Web comes from weave, just as speech comes from speak. Option A is only the -ing form, and B is a participle.

Q14How long ____ for the bus? (Fill in the blank)

(A) have you been waiting (B) you wait and look (C) you are waiting (D) you have been waiting

AnswerA — A question needs inversion (have + you).

Q15I have been working in this office for two weeks. I ____ no trouble so far.

(A) have had (B) have (C) had (D) am having

AnswerA — "So far" goes with the present perfect.

Q16I have had some trouble getting good stationery. I did shop at two places but I was disappointed. ____ was well-stocked.

(A) Either (B) Both (C) Neither (D) Each

AnswerC

Q17Rewrite the following sentence beginning with "What…": She is a very clever officer.

(A) What very clever officer she is. (B) What a very clever officer she is. (C) What cleverness she has as officer! (D) What a very clever officer she is!

AnswerD — An exclamation needs "What a…" and an exclamation mark.

Q18Spot the one correct sentence among the following:

(A) Have I to come to office every day? (B) It is hard to make you to understand simple things. (C) I have known him for a long time. (D) I must reply letters promptly.

AnswerC — B: make you understand (no "to"). D: reply to letters. A should be Do I have to…

Q19Rather than finding a ____ to a problem, some people delight in finding a problem to a ____. (Fill in the blanks)

(A) solution (B) solving (C) issue (D) way out

AnswerA

Q20Spot the odd word in the following group:

(A) Modernity (B) Moderate (C) Modernism (D) Modern

AnswerB — The other three come from "modern".

Q21What do we understand from the following sentence? The areas of town near the river are liable to flooding.

(A) These areas are often flooded. (B) These areas are lying low. (C) These areas need protection. (D) The rivers flood in these areas.

AnswerA — "Liable to" means likely to suffer something, often.

Q22In which of the following does "just" appear in the correct order?

(A) I just have returned from a wedding in Balasore. (B) I have returned just from a wedding in Balasore. (C) I have returned from just a wedding in Balasore. (D) I have just returned from a wedding in Balasore.

AnswerD — "Just" goes between have and the past participle.

(C) We have neither read the book nor visited the country. (D) Both the book we haven't read and the country we have not visited nevertheless exist.

(A) I unable to see the picture clearly. (B) I cannot hardly see the picture clearly. (C) I cannot able to see the picture clearly. (D) I can hardly see the picture clearly.

Q30Inflation is like sin; every government denounces it and every government practises it. Which of the following readings of this statement is more or less correct?

(A) Inflation is a sin, but a government hardly knows this. (B) Inflation is not a sin, but when a government practises it, it becomes sinful. (C) Inflation is sinful but every government finds it inescapable. (D) Inflation is least sinful when every government practises it.

Q34The boys are tired. What would be the next logical and correct sentence?

Q35Democratic nations naturally stand more in need of forms than other nations, and they naturally respect them less. What do "they" and "them" refer to?

(A) Forms, democratic nations (B) Forms, democracies (C) Democratic nations, forms (D) Democratic nations, other nations

AnswerC

Q36I am very tired. I'd rather ____.

(A) work more (B) not to work more (C) work anymore (D) not work anymore

AnswerD — "Would rather" takes the bare infinitive, and "anymore" needs a negative.

Q37I think I'll wait ____ Saturday before I decide what to do.

(A) till (B) before (C) unto (D) until

AnswerA or D — "till" and "until" mean the same here. If only one is allowed, "until" is the more formal choice.

Q38There was ____ concern about frequent power failure in the city.

(A) lots of (B) a lot of (C) a great deal of (D) very much

AnswerC — "A great deal of" is the standard formal choice with an uncountable noun like concern. B is acceptable in speech.

Q39____ the enquiry, the official maintained that nothing illegal had been done.

(A) In (B) Upon (C) At (D) Since

AnswerC — at the enquiry (during the hearing).

Q40I shall look through your report when I have a spare ____.

(A) time (B) moment (C) minute (D) occasion

AnswerB — "A spare moment" is the fixed phrase. "Time" cannot take "a".

Q41What ____ does it make if you don't buy a car?

(A) difference (B) change (C) matter (D) advantage

AnswerA

Q43Put this in direct speech: He asked me whether I would attend that day's meeting.

(A) "Will I attend that day's meeting?" he said to me. (B) "Will you attend today's meeting?" he said to me. (C) "Whether you are attending meeting," he said to me. (D) "When will you attend the meeting?" he said to me.

Q44Which of the following is an incorrect sentence?

(A) Hari is not used to the English weather. (B) Hari is used to speaking Bangla all the time. (C) Hari is getting used as living in Odisha. (D) Hari is not used to cooking his own food.

AnswerC — It should be getting used to living.

Q45Danny, you're late! You are going to miss your train, you ____.

Q51It takes ____ cars than ten boats to ferry them to the destination. (Fill in the blank)

Q53Rewrite this sentence using "let": Most schools do not allow their students to leave before 4 P.M.

(A) Most schools do not let their students leave before 4 P.M. (B) Most schools let students stay longer than 4 P.M. (C) Most students do not let their schools leave before 4 P.M. (D) Most schools do not let stay longer than 4 P.M.

AnswerA — "let + object + bare infinitive".

Q54Complete the sentence using the right form of the verb within brackets: Mahesh wishes he (live) with his parents in his own flat.

(A) Mahesh wishes that his parents lived with him in his own flat. (B) Mahesh wishes that his own flat for his parents' life. (C) Mahesh wishes he lived his own parents in his flat. (D) Mahesh wishes that he lived with his parents in his own flat.

AnswerD — "Wish" + past tense for an unreal present.

Q55Identify the sentence that carries the incorrect phrase:

(A) Harish's two sons take after him. (B) Harish cannot take on more work. (C) Harish has taken off in a good job. (D) Harish is taken ill and won't come.

AnswerC — It should be taken up a good job. (D is awkward, since "has been taken ill" is better, but "taken ill" is itself a correct phrase.)

Q56"I beg your pardon." When someone says so, what is meant is that:

(A) the speaker hasn't quite heard what you said (B) the speaker hasn't quite understood what you said (C) the speaker is apologizing for some wrong he has done (D) the speaker has neither heard nor quite understood you

AnswerA — As a question, "I beg your pardon?" asks you to repeat.

Koshish (meaning "making an effort") started as a student initiative, a project on homelessness and destitution. It was trying hard to decriminalize begging. It is shameful that we have a law that punishes the poor for being poor, with detention up to 10 years. It is the best evidence of apathy that we, as a society, have towards the poor. Q100In reported speech, how will this read? "I do not want to be forced into believing that God exists," said the girl. "If God indeed exists as you assert, show me, at least once, where I shall see Him like I see you now."

(B) The girl explained that she be forced not into believing that God exists, and if indeed He does, as I asserted, she demands to be shown at least once, where she would see Him like she saw me then. (C) The girl told me that she did not want to be forced into believing, and that if indeed God exists, as I assert, she demanded to be shown Him, at least once, where she would see Him like she sees me now.
