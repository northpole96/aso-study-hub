Each paper has 100 questions, so number of questions = percent of the paper.
Every question was tagged to one item of the official 2026-27 syllabus (OPSC-ASO-2026-27-Official-Syllabus.md):

(i) Grammar: Verb, Preposition, Adverb, Subject Verb Agreement, Error Correction / Recognition, Tenses, Fill in the Blanks with Articles etc., Vocabulary, Synonyms, Antonyms. (ii) Comprehension (iii) Sentence Rearrangement

Anything that fits none of these is listed as Not named in syllabus. The tags are my judgement; a few questions touch two items, and each was counted once, under its main skill.

The 2025 paper was the first to stay completely inside the printed syllabus. This is the same shift the GS paper made: from a loose quiz to a syllabus test.

4. What this means for 2027

  • Safe core (always asked): tenses, prepositions, error spotting, subject–verb agreement and vocabulary. Together they made 50–60% of every paper.
  • Rising: sentence rearrangement (1 → 5 → 20) and comprehension (4 → 5 → 10). Expect 2025 levels or close to them.
  • Adverb jumped to 15 in 2025. Most of these were inversion openers (No sooner, Hardly, Never before, So + adverb) and -ly forms.
  • Synonyms and antonyms are named in the syllabus but were absent in 2025. They were 11% in 2021. They could return, probably in small numbers (about 5–10).
  • Drop: narration, transformation, question tags, parts of speech and analogies. None of them is in the syllabus, and none appeared in 2025.
