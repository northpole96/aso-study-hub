What I found when I reviewed the old guide

I read the old guide line by line against the three question papers. Most of it is correct. These points were wrong, weak or missing, and they are fixed in this new guide.

Corrected from the old guide

  • "27 modes of corruption" (2021 Q42). The old guide gave Santhanam. Textbooks credit this list to the Central Vigilance Commission. Santhanam is the committee that led to the CVC.

Five lessons from the papers

1. The paper has moved from "news quiz" to "syllabus test". In 2018, about one question in four was pure news: Miss India, Ranji Trophy, a film award. In 2025 only a handful were like that. The examiner now takes each syllabus topic and asks from it. This is good news for you, because syllabus topics can be learned from a guide. Random news cannot.

2. News is still there, but it is attached to a topic. The 2025 paper had about 30 questions that needed recent knowledge. Almost all of them sat inside a syllabus topic: defence exercises under Defence, a Supreme Court ruling under Polity, a turtle count under Odisha. So when you read news, ask "which syllabus topic does this belong to?" If it belongs to none, it is unlikely to be asked.

3. Questions have become longer. In 2018 nearly every question was one line. In 2025 about 31 of 100 were "multi-step" questions.

Chapter 01 · 11 to 18 questions

Indian Constitution (Polity)

Polity is the safest chapter in the paper. The questions ask two kinds of things. First, where something is written: which Article, which Schedule, which Amendment. 1.1 How the Constitution was made

A body called the Constituent Assembly wrote the Constitution. It was set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946.

  • 9 December 1946: first meeting. Sachchidananda Sinha was the temporary chairman. Dr Rajendra Prasad was then elected permanent President of the Assembly.
  • 13 December 1946: Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution. It later became the Preamble.
  • 29 August 1947: the Drafting Committee was formed. Dr B.R. Ambedkar was its chairman. B.N. Rau was the constitutional adviser.
  • 26 November 1949: the Constitution was adopted. This is Constitution Day.
  • 26 January 1950: it came into force. This date was chosen because on 26 January 1930 the Congress had celebrated "Purna Swaraj Day" 2025 Q54.

The work took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.

1.2 The Preamble

The Preamble is the opening paragraph. It says what kind of country India is and what it promises to its people.

  • India is a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic.
  • It promises Justice (social, economic, political), Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.
  • The words Socialist, Secular and Integrity were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976 2025 Q32. This is the only time the Preamble was changed.

Is the Preamble part of the Constitution? In the Berubari case (1960) the Supreme Court said no. In the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) it said yes. So today: the Preamble is part of the Constitution, it can be amended (without damaging the basic structure), but you cannot go to court to enforce it.

Trap

The 2018 paper asked "what is NOT correct about the Preamble" 2018 Q34 and gave two false options: "it cannot be amended" and "it is enforceable in a court". Both are false. Remember both facts: it can be amended; it cannot be enforced.

1.4 Fundamental Rights (Part III, Articles 12 to 35)

These are rights you can enforce in court. There are six groups. The Article ranges are asked directly 2025 Q82.

RightArticlesMain points
Equality14 to 18Equal before law (14), no discrimination (15), equal chance in government jobs (16), untouchability ended (17), titles ended (18)
Freedom19 to 22Six freedoms (19), protection when accused (20), life and liberty (21), education for ages 6 to 14 (21A), protection on arrest (22)
Against exploitation23 to 24No forced labour or trafficking (23), no child labour in factories and mines (24)
Religion25 to 28Freedom to follow and spread religion, manage religious affairs, no tax for a religion, no religious teaching in government schools
Culture and education29 to 30Minorities can protect their language and run their own schools
Constitutional remedies32Go straight to the Supreme Court if a right is broken

Three points that were asked:

  • Property is no longer a Fundamental Right. The 44th Amendment (1978) removed Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31 2021 Q39. Property is now only a legal right under Article 300A.
  • Who can put "reasonable restrictions" on the freedoms? Only the legislature, by making a law. In the exam the answer is Parliament 2021 Q32.
  • Article 21 keeps growing. Courts have read many rights into "life and liberty". Privacy is one: in the Puttaswamy case (2017), nine judges led by Chief Justice J.S. Khehar agreed that privacy is a Fundamental Right. It is not absolute; a fair law can limit it 2018 Q16. In 2025 the Supreme Court said maternity leave is part of a woman's reproductive rights under Article 21, and it can be given even for a third child 2025 Q6.

Is voting a Fundamental Right? No. The right to vote comes from Article 326 and election law. The exam options were Fundamental, Natural, Constitutional and Legal 2021 Q34. It is definitely not Fundamental or Natural. Courts have called it a legal (statutory) right and, more recently, a constitutional right. Answer keys differ, so do not lose sleep over this one.

The five writs. A writ is a court order. The Supreme Court issues them under Article 32 and High Courts under Article 226.

Article 35A protected special rights of permanent residents of Jammu and Kashmir 2018 Q35. It ended when Article 370 was removed on 5 August 2019. Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh, became Union Territories on 31 October 2019.

1.7 President, Vice-President, Prime Minister

President (Articles 52 to 62)

  • Minimum age 35. Term 5 years.
  • Elected by an electoral college: elected MPs of both Houses + elected MLAs of all states, Delhi and Puducherry (Article 54).
  • Method: proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot. This is in Article 55(3) 2021 Q36.
  • Removed by impeachment (Article 61) for "violation of the Constitution".
  • Can pardon (Article 72) and issue ordinances when Parliament is not sitting (Article 123).
  • Addresses Parliament at the first session after each general election and at the first session of every year (Article 87) 2018 Q66.

Vice-President (Articles 63 to 71)

  • Elected by all members of both Houses, elected and nominated. MLAs do not vote 2018 Q36.
  • He is the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • The present Vice-President is C.P. Radhakrishnan, elected in September 2025 after Jagdeep Dhankhar resigned. The President is Droupadi Murmu.

Memory trick

President: MLAs in, nominated out. Vice-President: nominated in, MLAs out.

Prime Minister and Council of Ministers (Articles 74 and 75) 2025 Q18

  • The President appoints the PM. Other ministers are appointed on the PM's advice. The PM gives them their departments (portfolios).
  • The PM can be from either House. Indira Gandhi and Manmohan Singh were Rajya Sabha members. So "the PM must be a Lok Sabha member" is false.
  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
  • A person who is not an MP can become minister or PM, but must become an MP within six months.
  • The 91st Amendment (2003) says the Council of Ministers cannot be bigger than 15% of the Lok Sabha 2025 Q91. The same 15% rule applies in states (minimum 12 ministers).

Attorney General (Article 76): the top law officer of the Government of India. Appointed by the President. He can speak in Parliament but cannot vote. The 2018 paper printed "Article 1976" as a trap 2018 Q90.

The first Speaker of the Lok Sabha was G.V. Mavalankar 2018 Q79. The Speaker decides whether a bill is a Money Bill, chairs a joint sitting, and decides defection cases.

Budget words

  • The Constitution calls the budget the Annual Financial Statement (Article 112).
  • Vote on Account: money given in advance so the government can run for about two months until the full budget is passed 2018 Q37.
  • Appropriation Bill: permission to take money out of the Consolidated Fund.
  • Finance Bill: the tax proposals.
  • Supplementary grant: extra money when the approved amount falls short.

Special powers of each House

  • Only Rajya Sabha 2025 Q59: (1) allow Parliament to make a law on a State List subject (Article 249); (2) allow the creation of a new All India Service (Article 312). Both need a two-thirds vote.
  • Only Lok Sabha: Money Bills and no-confidence motions.
  • Both Houses together: electing the Vice-President, creating or ending Legislative Councils, changing state boundaries.

Trap

"How can a new All India Service be created?" 2021 Q45. It takes two steps: first a Rajya Sabha resolution, then Parliament makes the law. The first step is what makes it special, so the usual answer is a resolution of the Rajya Sabha.

1.9 Courts

  • The Supreme Court has the Chief Justice + 33 judges = 34. Supreme Court judges retire at 65, High Court judges at 62.
  • Judges are chosen through the collegium (a group of senior judges).
  • The 99th Amendment tried to replace the collegium with a National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC). It came into force on 13 April 2015 2021 Q37 and was struck down by the Supreme Court in October 2015.
  • The present Chief Justice of India is Justice Surya Kant (53rd, since 24 November 2025). Check this before the exam, because his term ends in February 2027.

1.10 The States

  • The Governor is appointed by the President and stays "during the pleasure of the President". The Sarkaria Commission said Governors should be well-known people from outside the state who are not active in politics, that is, non-partisan 2025 Q89.
  • Legislative Council (the upper house in a state). Only six states have one: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka. Odisha has none.
  • To create or abolish a Council, the State Assembly passes a resolution and then Parliament makes a law (Article 169) 2021 Q35.
  • Minimum age for a Council member is 30 2025 Q33.
  • Delhi became the National Capital Territory by the 69th Amendment (1991) 2018 Q75.

Local government

  • Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) suggested three-level Panchayati Raj: village, block, district. Rajasthan started first (Nagaur, 2 October 1959). This committee is about panchayats, not civil services 2025 Q41.
  • 73rd Amendment: panchayats, 11th Schedule, in force 24 April 1993 (National Panchayati Raj Day).
  • 74th Amendment: municipalities, 12th Schedule, in force 1 June 1993 2018 Q40.
  • Local body elections are run by the State Election Commission, not the Election Commission of India 2025 Q34.

1.12 National Human Rights Commission

This came in all three papers. Learn all of it.

  • Law: Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993. It is a statutory body (made by a law, not by the Constitution).
  • Chairperson: a former Chief Justice of India 2018 Q65. Since 2019, a former Supreme Court judge can also be chosen.
  • First chairperson: Justice Ranganath Mishra 2021 Q51 (he was from Odisha).
  • Who selects: a committee of six. The Prime Minister (head), Lok Sabha Speaker, Rajya Sabha Deputy Chairman, Leaders of Opposition of both Houses, and the Home Minister. The Lok Sabha's Deputy Speaker is not in it 2025 Q87.
  • Term: 3 years or age 70.
  • Power: it can inquire and recommend. Its advice is not binding.

2.5 Tools for openness (transparency)

Right to Information Act, 2005

  • It began as a village movement in Rajasthan in the 1990s. A group called MKSS asked to see how village money was spent.

  • Any citizen can ask a Public Information Officer for information.

3.2 Measuring the economy

  • GDP (Gross Domestic Product): value of all final goods and services made inside India in one year.
  • GNP: GDP + income Indians earn abroad − income foreigners earn in India.
  • "Net" always means wear and tear (depreciation) has been taken out.
  • Nominal means at today's prices. Real means price rise has been removed.
  • Per capita means per person.

So the best measure of whether people are really better off is real income per person 2018 Q52.

Who makes what: services give more than half of India's output, industry about a quarter, and farming under a fifth 2025 Q21. But farming still gives work to the largest number of people. Small output, many workers: this gap is the main problem of the Indian economy.

India became the world's fourth largest economy in 2025, moving past Japan. The base year for GDP was changed to 2022-23 in February 2026.

4.1 How the British took India

Read this as a story in five steps.

Step 1: Bengal. In the Battle of Plassey (1757) Robert Clive beat Siraj-ud-Daulah, because Mir Jafar changed sides. In the Battle of Buxar (1764) the British beat three rulers together: Mir Qasim, the Nawab of Awadh and the Mughal emperor. After this the Company got the right to collect taxes (Diwani) in Bengal, Bihar and Odisha in 1765.

Step 2: The French. The British and French fought three Carnatic Wars in South India. The Battle of Wandiwash (1760) ended the French challenge 2018 Q26.

Step 3: Mysore. Four wars against Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan. Tipu died fighting at Seringapatam in 1799.

Step 4: The Marathas. In the Treaty of Bassein (1802) the weak Peshwa Baji Rao II accepted British protection. Historians say the British signed a treaty with a "cypher", meaning a zero, a man with no real power 2018 Q71. In the war that followed, the British took Odisha in 1803 from the Bhonsle rulers of Nagpur. It was handed over by the Treaty of Deogaon. Argaon was a battle in that war, not the treaty 2018 Q27.

Step 5: Punjab. After two Anglo-Sikh wars, Punjab was taken in 1849. The boy king Duleep Singh was sent to England.

Two famous tricks for taking states

  • Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley, 1798): an Indian ruler kept British soldiers in his state and paid for them. Hyderabad was the first to accept.
  • Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie): if a ruler died without a son of his own, the British took the state. Satara, Jhansi and Nagpur went this way. Awadh was taken in 1856 for "bad government".

Rulers and their states 2021 Q21 2021 Q22: Gulab Singh, Jammu · Duleep Singh, Punjab · Wajid Ali Shah, Awadh · Krishnaraja Wodeyar, Mysore · the Nizam, Hyderabad · Govinda Chandra, Cachar. Shah Shuja and Zaman Shah were rulers of Afghanistan, so pairing them with Rohilkhand or Peshawar is wrong. The Bhangi clan (misl) ruled part of Punjab 2021 Q25.

5.1 Inside the Earth

The Earth is like a boiled egg. The crust is the thin shell. The mantle is the thick white part, made of hot rock that moves very slowly. The core is the yolk, made of iron and nickel. The outer core is liquid and the inner core is solid.

Earthquakes 2018 Q43

  • They are caused by forces inside the Earth (endogenetic forces).
  • Focus: the point under the ground where the rock breaks.
  • Epicentre: the point on the surface just above the focus.
  • Richter scale measures the energy (magnitude). Mercalli scale measures the damage (intensity).

Memory trick

Epi = upon. The epicentre is upon the surface. The focus is below.

6.3 The land

The Himalayas are young fold mountains with three rows: the Greater Himalaya (highest; Kanchenjunga is India's highest peak), the Lesser Himalaya (hill stations such as Shimla), and the Shiwaliks (the outer, lowest row).

The Northern Plain was made by soil brought by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra.

The Peninsular Plateau is the oldest part of India.

  • The Aravallis are the oldest fold mountains; the answer to "where are the oldest rocks" among the usual options 2018 Q47.
  • The Vindhya Range divides the Ganga plain from the Deccan Plateau 2018 Q73. The Satpura runs to its south.
  • The Western Ghats are a continuous wall. Highest peak: Anamudi.
  • The Eastern Ghats are broken by rivers.
  • South of the Palghat gap lie the Anamalai, Palani and Cardamom Hills. The Cardamom Hills are the southernmost, nearest to Kanyakumari 2025 Q12.

Rocks by age. The oldest are the Archaean rocks, then the Dharwar system (the oldest of the "geosynclines" in the question 2021 Q71; rich in iron and gold), then Cuddapah, Vindhyan, Gondwana (almost all of India's coal) and the Deccan Traps (lava that turned into black soil).

6.4 Rivers

  • Himalayan rivers (Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra) flow all year, fed by snow and rain.
  • Peninsular rivers depend on rain.

7.4 Chilika and other lakes

Chilika lies in Puri, Khurda and Ganjam districts. It is the largest brackish-water (half-salty) lagoon in Asia.

FIG_018Schematic plan of Chilika lagoon, its sand bar, mouth and islands.

  • Why is it a lagoon? Sea waves piled up sand and built a long bar. This bar cut a piece of the sea off from the ocean 2018 Q45. A narrow mouth still joins it to the Bay of Bengal.
  • It was one of India's first Ramsar sites (1981).
  • It was put on the Montreux Record (a danger list of wetlands) in 1993 and taken off in 2002 after it was repaired. It was the first site in Asia to come off the list.

7.5 Climate, soils, forests

Cyclones. Odisha's coast is one of the most cyclone-hit places in the world. The Super Cyclone of 1999 killed about 10,000 people. After it, Odisha set up OSDMA, the first state disaster authority in India. Later cyclones: Phailin (2013), Titli (2018), Fani (2019, Puri), Yaas (2021), Dana (October 2024). Soils. Red and yellow soil covers the most land. Laterite is found on high, rain-washed land (Khurda, the Eastern Ghats). Alluvial soil is in the deltas. Black soil is in parts of the south-west.

Trap

Endemic = found only in one place. Endangered = close to dying out. Epidemic = a disease spreading fast. The 2025 paper put all three in the options.

Three levels of diversity 2021 Q97

  • Alpha: variety inside one community.
  • Beta: how much variety changes from one community to the next.
  • Gamma: total variety of a whole region.

Memory trick

A-B-G = small to big. Alpha is one place, Beta is between places, Gamma is the grand total.

Parasitic plants 2021 Q95. A hemiparasite steals water from its host but still makes its own food with green leaves (mistletoe). A holoparasite makes no food at all (dodder, Rafflesia).

8.2 Food chains and energy

FIG_019Isometric energy pyramid with ten percent transfer.

  • Producers (green plants) → herbivores → carnivores → decomposers (bacteria and fungi).
  • 10% law (Lindeman): only about 10% of energy passes to the next level. So food chains are short.
  • The pyramid of energy is always upright.
  • Assimilation efficiency means how much of the eaten food is actually absorbed. It is low in plant-eaters, because plant fibre is hard to digest. It is high in meat-eaters. Predatory spiders absorb over 90% 2021 Q100.
  • Biomagnification: poisons like DDT and mercury get stronger at each step up the food chain.
  • Eutrophication: fertiliser washes into a lake, algae grow wildly, oxygen falls and fish die.

8.3 Saving biodiversity

Hotspots. The idea came from Norman Myers in 1988 2021 Q94. A hotspot is very rich in endemic plants and has already lost most of its natural cover. The world has 36. India has 4: the Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Western Ghats–Sri Lanka, and Sundaland (Nicobar Islands).

IUCN Red List. The IUCN (head office at Gland, Switzerland) lists how close a species is to dying out.

Extinct → Extinct in the Wild → Critically Endangered → Endangered → Vulnerable → Near Threatened → Least Concern.

The middle three together are called "threatened". Examples: Great Indian Bustard, critically endangered; tiger, endangered; olive ridley turtle, vulnerable.

Bramble Cay melomys 2025 Q100. A small rat that lived on one tiny island in Australia's Great Barrier Reef. Rising sea water washed over the island again and again and destroyed its plants. It is the first mammal known to die out because of man-made climate change.

9.5 Engines and new technology

Ramjet and scramjet 2025 Q51

FIG_022Long section of a scramjet engine with intake, combustor and nozzle.

  • A normal jet engine has spinning blades that squeeze the air.
  • A ramjet has no moving parts. The speed of the vehicle itself squeezes the air.
  • A scramjet is a ramjet in which the fuel burns while the air is still moving faster than sound (supersonic burning). It works only at very high, hypersonic speeds (above five times the speed of sound). It is useless at slow speeds.

12.2 A simple way to collect news

  1. Use one source only. One monthly current affairs magazine in English, plus one Odisha news source. More sources only mean more to revise.
  2. Write by topic, not by date. Keep one page each for Defence, Days, Schemes, Odisha, Summits, Reports, Courts, Science. When you read "India and Japan held Exercise X", it goes on the Defence page.

13.2 How to revise so that it stays

  • Close the page and say it. Reading again feels easy but does little. Covering the answer and recalling it is what builds memory. That is why the blue boxes hide the answers.
  • Keep an error notebook. Every question you get wrong in a past paper goes in, with the right fact in one line. Read this notebook every Sunday.
  • Space it out. See a chapter on day 1, day 3, day 10 and day 30. Four short looks beat one long one.
  • Learn tables as pairs. Viceroy and event, exercise and country, place and mineral. The exam asks for the pair.

13.3 In the exam hall

You have 90 minutes for 100 questions. That is under a minute each, so do three rounds.

  1. Round 1 (about 40 minutes): answer everything you know at once. Skip the rest, but put a small mark in the question booklet.
  2. Round 2 (about 30 minutes): work on the marked questions by cutting out wrong options.
  3. Round 3 (the rest): check that the bubbles match the question numbers. Do not change answers without a clear reason.

When should you guess? A right answer gives +1. A wrong answer gives −0.25.

What you knowShould you answer?
You can remove two wrong optionsYes, always. On average you gain
You can remove one wrong optionYes
You know nothing about itLeave it. The gain is almost zero and you risk a minus

How to crack a statement question. Take the ITBP question of 2025 as a model. It gave three statements and options like "1 and 2", "2 and 3", "1 and 3", "all three".

  • You know for sure that ITBP was formed after the 1962 war. So statement 2 is true. Cross out "1 and 3".
  • Statement 1 has very exact details: a place name, a state, a purpose. Such statements are where examiners hide a change. Doubt it.
  • That leaves "2 and 3". You got the answer by knowing one fact well.

Words that often make a statement false: "only", "exclusively", "always", "must", "all", "never". In 2025, "TROPEX focuses exclusively on the Western Fleet" and "the Prime Minister must always be a member of the Lok Sabha" were both false.

Where examiners hide the change: a swapped year (1978 for 1948), a swapped place (Vienna for Geneva, Daya for Devi, Rajasthan for Chhattisgarh), a swapped person (Amherst for Macaulay, Motilal Nehru for Patel), or a swapped word (Green Economy for Blue Economy). When you study, notice the year, place and person in each fact.

In short

Know one fact for sure, cut the options, and do not guess blind. Strong Polity, History, Defence and Environment will carry the paper.
