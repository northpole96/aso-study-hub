#CH inversion | Inversion and joining words | GRAMMAR · Rules 101–110
In a normal sentence the subject comes before the verb: "She **had** never seen such a sight." When a **negative or limiting word** moves to the front for force, English swaps the order, and the helping verb jumps before the subject: "Never **had she** seen such a sight." This is called **inversion**. In 2025 it gave seven marks ⟦2025 Q8⟧ ⟦2025 Q11⟧ ⟦2025 Q21⟧ ⟦2025 Q40⟧ ⟦2025 Q70⟧, and the pairs of joining words (no sooner … than, not only … but also) were tested alongside it.

>KEY:The inversion pattern
Negative word + helping verb (do, does, did, had, have, is, can, will) + subject + main verb. If the plain sentence has no helping verb, add do, does or did: "He rarely **smiles**" → "Rarely **does** he **smile**."

##R101 No sooner … than || No sooner + had + subject + V3 … than; never when or then
"No sooner" is a comparative, so its partner is **than**, never "when" or "then". The verb after it is inverted, usually with **had + V3** in the past: "No sooner **had** he reached the station **than** the train left." In the present, use do or does.
✗ No sooner did the child touch the tree when it broke into blossom.
✓ No sooner did the child touch the tree than it broke out into blossoms. ⟦2025 Q8⟧
✗ No sooner he had left than it began to rain.
✓ No sooner had he left than it began to rain.

##R102 Hardly and scarcely … when || Hardly or scarcely + had + subject + V3 … when (or before); never than
**Hardly, scarcely** and **barely** pair with **when** (or before), not than. They also cause inversion: "Hardly **had** I sat down **when** the bell rang." On its own at the start, hardly still inverts: "Hardly **does** one encounter such a feat." ⟦2025 Q11⟧
✗ Hardly had I reached than it started raining.
✓ Hardly had I reached when it started raining.

##R103 Negative openers || Never, never before, seldom, rarely, little, nowhere, at no time, under no circumstances, not until + inversion
These words at the front of a sentence always cause inversion: **never, never before, seldom, rarely, little, nowhere, at no time, on no account, under no circumstances, in no way, not once, not until, only then, only after, only when**. ⟦2025 Q40⟧
✗ Never before such data had been accessible.
✓ Never before had such data been accessible to researchers. ⟦2025 Q40⟧
✗ Little he knew that he was being watched.
✓ Little did he know that he was being watched.
✓ Only then did I realise my mistake.

##R104 So and such at the front || So + adjective or adverb + verb + subject … that; such + be + noun … that
When **so + adjective/adverb** begins the sentence, the verb inverts and **that** follows: "So compellingly **did she** articulate her views **that** the audience sat in silence." ⟦2025 Q21⟧ With **such**, the pattern is "Such **was** his anger **that** …". Never write "such compelling … that" when an adverb is needed.
✗ Such compelling did she speak that everyone listened.
✓ So compellingly did she speak that everyone listened.

##R105 Not only … but also || Keep both halves parallel and in the same tense; at the front, not only causes inversion
**Not only … but also** must join two things of the **same kind** (two verbs, two nouns, two clauses). When "not only" opens the sentence, it inverts the first half: "Not only **did** the rain ruin the picnic, but the wind also made it impossible …" Both halves must be in the same tense. ⟦2025 Q70⟧
✗ Not only does the rain ruin the picnic, but also the wind made it impossible to set up the tent.
✓ Not only did the rain ruin the picnic, but the wind also made it impossible to set up the tent. ⟦2025 Q70⟧
✗ He not only lost his money but also his health.
✓ He lost not only his money but also his health.

##R106 Correlative pairs || Either … or, neither … nor, both … and, whether … or, though … yet, so … that, as … as, such … as
These words come in **fixed pairs**. Never swap a partner.
|Opening word|Its partner|Example|
|---|---|---|
|either|or|Either you or he must go.|
|neither|nor|Neither he nor I knew.|
|both|and|Both Ram and Shyam came.|
|not only|but also|not only clever but also kind|
|no sooner|than|No sooner had … than …|
|hardly, scarcely|when|Hardly had … when …|
|though, although|yet (or nothing), never but|Though he is poor, yet he is honest.|
|so|that|so tired that he slept|
|such|that, or as (= like)|such men as these|
|as|as (positive), so … as (negative)|as tall as; not so tall as|
|whether|or|whether you like it or not|
|lest|should|lest he should fall|
|rather|than|I would rather die than beg.|
|the same|as, or that|the same book as yours|
✗ Though he is poor, but he is honest.
✓ Though he is poor, yet he is honest.

##R107 Because, because of, due to, despite || Because + clause; because of, owing to, despite, in spite of + noun or -ing
**Because, since, as, though, although** are followed by a **clause** (a subject and a verb). **Because of, owing to, due to, despite, in spite of** are followed by a **noun or -ing word**. "Despite" never takes "of"; "in spite" always does. ⟦2021 Q12⟧
✗ They could not admire him because of they dislike him.
✓ They could not admire him because they dislike him. ⟦2021 Q12⟧
✗ Despite of the rain, the match went on.
✓ Despite the rain, the match went on. (or: In spite of the rain …)

##R108 Linking adverbs show the turn of thought || However, but = contrast; therefore, so, consequently = result; moreover, also = addition
A **linking adverb** tells you which way the thought turns. **Contrast:** however, but, yet, still, nevertheless, on the other hand, whereas ⟦2021 Q26⟧. **Result:** therefore, so, thus, hence, consequently. **Addition:** moreover, furthermore, besides, also. **Example:** for instance. **Time:** first, then, finally, initially. Read both sentences, decide the turn, then pick the word. ⟦2025 Q33⟧ ⟦2025 Q52⟧ These same words are the best clues in paragraph rearrangement (Chapter 14).
✗ The first experiment failed. Therefore, the team learned valuable lessons.
✓ The initial experiment failed. However, the team learned valuable lessons. ⟦2025 Q33⟧
✓ French people love cooking, whereas the English don't seem very interested. ⟦2021 Q26⟧

##R109 Parallel structure || Words joined by and, or, but, than must be the same form: to read and to write; reading and writing
Things joined by **and, or, but, than, as** or a pair like **both … and** must have the **same grammatical shape**: two nouns, two -ing forms, two to-verbs, two clauses. "She enjoys hiking and partying." ⟦2025 Q75⟧
✗ He likes reading, writing and to swim.
✓ He likes reading, writing and swimming.
✗ It is better to give than receiving.
✓ It is better to give than to receive.

##R110 Till, until, as soon as, so that || Until and till already include not-until meaning; as soon as has no then; so that takes may or can
**Until** and **till** mean "up to the time when"; do not add "not" inside the clause they open. ⟦2018 Q37⟧ **As soon as** needs no "then" after it. **So that** (purpose) takes **may, might, can** or **could**: "He works hard so that he **may** succeed." **In order to** takes a base verb.
✗ Wait here until I do not return.
✓ Wait here until I return.
✗ As soon as he came, then the meeting began.
✓ As soon as he came, the meeting began.

#CH prepositions | Prepositions and phrasal verbs | GRAMMAR · Rules 111–120
A preposition shows how one word is linked to another: in, on, at, of, for, with, from, to, by. English prepositions follow **habit**, not logic, so they must be learnt in pairs: "afraid **of**", "good **at**", "comply **with**". The 2021 paper asked ten in a row ⟦2021 Q53⟧ to ⟦2021 Q62⟧, plus multi-blank sets, and OPSC reused a 2018 preposition question word for word in 2021 ⟦2021 Q9⟧ ⟦2018 Q87⟧. Learn the tables in this chapter by saying them aloud.

##R111 At, on, in for time || At + clock time; on + day or date; in + month, year, season, part of day
**At** for an exact time: at 5 o'clock, at noon, at night, at the end of the lesson ⟦2021 Q60⟧. **On** for days and dates: on Monday, on 15 August, on my birthday. **In** for longer periods: in June, in 2027, in winter, in the morning. **By** means "not later than": by Friday. **Until/till** means "up to": until Friday. **Within** means "before the end of": within a week. **In time** = early enough; **on time** = at the planned time.
✗ The exam is in 17 January.
✓ The exam is on 17 January.
✓ The teacher set some homework at the end of the lesson. ⟦2021 Q60⟧

##R112 At, on, in for place; between and among; beside and besides || At a point, on a surface, in an enclosed space; between two, among many; beside = next to, besides = in addition to
**At** for a point: at the door, at the station, at work. **On** for a surface or a line: on the table, on the wall, on the road. **In** for an inside space or a big area: in the room, in Odisha, in an apartment ⟦2021 Q38⟧. **Between** for two, **among** for more than two. **Beside** means next to; **besides** means in addition to. ⟦2018 Q62⟧
✗ The money was divided between the four brothers.
✓ The money was divided among the four brothers.
✓ He sat beside me; besides us, there were two others. ⟦2018 Q62⟧

These adjectives always take the same preposition. All of the bold ones were asked in the three papers.
|Preposition|Adjectives and participles|

|for|**eligible** for ⟦2018 Q5⟧, famous for, responsible for, sorry for, ready for, **notorious** for ⟦2021 Q18⟧|

##R115 No preposition after these verbs || Discuss, enter, reach, resemble, marry, attack, order, request, approach, investigate, accompany: no preposition
These verbs take their object **directly**. Adding about, into, to, with or on is a common error: **discuss** a problem, **enter** a room, **reach** home, **resemble** his father, **marry** her, **attack** the enemy, **order** a meal, **request** help, **approach** the officer, **investigate** a case, **accompany** a friend, **inform** them (of something), **consider** him (no "as" ⟦2018 Q98⟧).
✗ We discussed about the problem.
✓ We discussed the problem.
✗ He reached to Delhi at night.
✓ He reached Delhi at night.
✗ I will inform this to them.
✓ I will inform them of this. ⟦2021 Q37⟧

##R116 Noun + preposition || Expertise in, research on, experiment on, access to, approval for, demand for, respect for, in honour of, with regard to

✗ He has no access of the files.
✓ He has no access to the files.
✓ They organised a party in his honour. ⟦2021 Q7⟧

##R118 Fixed phrases with prepositions || With regard to, in accordance with, on account of, in spite of, by mistake, on purpose, at random, take to heart
Many short phrases always use the same preposition: **with regard to** ⟦2021 Q5⟧, **in accordance with**, **on account of**, **in spite of**, **instead of**, **by means of**, **in addition to**, **on behalf of**, **by mistake**, **by chance**, **on purpose**, **at random**, **in vain**, **at once**, **on foot**, **by bus**, **under no circumstances** ⟦2018 Q86⟧, **take something to heart** ⟦2021 Q55⟧.
✗ With regard of his principles, he must be careful.
✓ With regard to his principles, he has to be very careful. ⟦2021 Q5⟧

##R119 Prepositions that change the meaning || Angry with a person, at a thing; agree with a person, to a plan; die of a disease, from a cause
Some words take **different prepositions for different meanings**. Learn them as pairs.
|Word|With a person|With a thing or idea|
|---|---|---|
|angry|angry **with** him|angry **at** his rudeness|
|agree|agree **with** you|agree **to** the proposal|
|die|die **of** a disease (cholera)|die **from** a wound or cause (overwork)|
|compare|compare **with** (find likeness and difference)|compare **to** (say one is like the other)|
|tired|tired **of** (bored)|tired **from** (worn out by)|
|decide|decide **on** (choose) ⟦2021 Q58⟧|decide **about** (think about)|
✗ He died from cholera.
✓ He died of cholera.

##R120 Prepositions of movement and travel || Into = movement inside; in = position; by bus, by train, on foot; to a place, towards a direction
**Into** shows movement from outside to inside: "He jumped **into** the river." **In** shows where something already is: "He was swimming **in** the river." Travel takes **by**: by bus, by train, by air; but **on foot**, and **in my car**. **To** shows the end point: go to school; **towards** shows the direction only. **Arrive at** a small place, **arrive in** a city or country; but **reach** takes nothing (Rule 115). For "late by about an hour", the 2021 paper wanted "about". ⟦2021 Q62⟧ ⟨CHECK the blank would normally stay empty⟩
✗ He jumped in the well to save the child.
✓ He jumped into the well to save the child.
✗ We went there by foot.
✓ We went there on foot.
