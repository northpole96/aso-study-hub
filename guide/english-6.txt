#CH vocabulary | Vocabulary, synonyms and antonyms | SKILLS · 21 marks in 2025
Vocabulary was the **biggest single block** in 2025: 21 blanks where you choose the word that fits the meaning ⟦2025 Q2⟧ ⟦2025 Q45⟧ ⟦2025 Q51⟧. In 2021 there were also synonym and antonym questions ⟦2021 Q43⟧ to ⟦2021 Q47⟧. The syllabus names both synonyms and antonyms, so expect about 5 to 10 of them to come back. Unlike the grammar rules, you cannot learn every word. But you can learn the **method**, and the words the examiner likes.

##The clue-word method
Almost every vocabulary blank has a **clue** in the same sentence. Find it before you look at the options.
- **Contrast clues:** but, unlike, while, although, despite, whereas, yet. The blank means the **opposite** of the clue. "His ____ outlook is refreshing, **unlike** the pessimism of his brothers" → positive. ⟦2025 Q5⟧ "The weather was ____ yesterday, **but** today it feels chilly" → warm. ⟦2025 Q28⟧
- **Same-direction clues:** and, so, so … that, because, as, which means. The blank **agrees** with the clue. "His explanation was **so** ____ **that** everyone understood" → clear. ⟦2025 Q2⟧ "The house looked ____ **as** it had an unwelcoming air" → bleak. ⟦2025 Q17⟧
- **Definition clues:** a phrase after a comma or "which" explains the word. "We meet ____, **in June and in December**" → biannually (twice a year). ⟦2025 Q7⟧
- **Result clues:** "making the workplace enjoyable" → cheerful. ⟦2025 Q26⟧ "everyone was moved to tears" → touching. ⟦2025 Q58⟧

>TIP:Two options, one direction
In most 2025 blanks, two options were positive and two negative. Decide the direction from the clue, which removes two at once, and then choose the stronger fit.

#CH errors | Error spotting: the method | SKILLS · 6–12 marks a year
An error-spotting question gives one sentence in four parts. One part breaks a rule. The questions gave 7 marks in 2018, 12 in 2021 and 6 in 2025, but they really test every rule in Chapters 1 to 11 at once. The way to win is a **fixed order of checks**, so you never read the sentence aimlessly.

@@fig scan

##The six checks, in order
- **1. Find the subject.** Strip away "as well as", "along with", "of the …" and anything between commas. Is it one thing or many? (Rules 28–49)
- **2. Check its verb.** Does the verb match the subject you found? This alone solved four of the six 2025 questions: were → was ⟦2025 Q65⟧, feel → feels ⟦2025 Q67⟧, has → have ⟦2025 Q68⟧, are → is ⟦2025 Q69⟧.
- **3. Check the tense.** Look for time clues and for the other verbs in the sentence. Are they in matching tenses? "will have bought" in an if-sentence about the past ⟦2025 Q66⟧; "does" with "made" ⟦2025 Q70⟧.
- **4. Check pronouns.** Does each pronoun match its noun (its or their, one or his)? Is the case right (I or me, who or whom)?
- **5. Check articles and determiners.** a/an by sound; the with superlatives; each/every; few/a few.
- **6. Check prepositions and word forms.** Fixed pairs, extra prepositions after discuss or reach, adjective vs adverb, -ing vs to.

>WARN:Two errors in one sentence
Sometimes a careless sentence has two faults, as in ⟦2025 Q69⟧ (are and their) and ⟦2025 Q70⟧ (does and "in the slope"). Choose the **first clear grammar error** that the options point to, usually the verb. ⟨CHECK for 2025 Q70 option D is also defensible⟩

#CH rearrange | Sentence rearrangement | SKILLS · 20 marks in 2025
This part grew faster than any other: **1 question in 2018, 5 in 2021, 20 in 2025**. There are two kinds. In the first, four parts of one sentence (P, Q, R, S) must be put in order ⟦2025 Q71⟧ to ⟦2025 Q84⟧. In the second, four sentences must be put in order to make a paragraph ⟦2025 Q85⟧ to ⟦2025 Q90⟧. Both are pure grammar and logic, so with practice they become the fastest marks in the paper.

##Parts of one sentence (P, Q, R, S)
- **Find the subject first.** It is a noun or pronoun with no word before it that needs one: "she has made", "the teacher bought", "not everyone". ⟦2025 Q77⟧
- **Join the subject to its verb.** The verb part usually starts with has, was, will, or a past form: "The team (P) was ecstatic (S)". ⟦2025 Q74⟧
- **Put the object right after the verb.** "painted a vivid description of the scene". ⟦2025 Q81⟧
- **Time and place phrases go last**: "over the years", "yesterday", "at the conference". ⟦2025 Q71⟧ ⟦2025 Q82⟧ ⟦2025 Q84⟧ They can also open the sentence: "Last semester I took a course …" ⟦2025 Q76⟧
- **A part ending in "and", "of" or "the" must be followed** by a part that completes it. "the complexities of" + "modern technology". ⟦2025 Q77⟧
- **Use the options.** Fix the first two parts, then look for the one option that starts that way. You often need not order all four.

>WARN:When no option fits
Once in 2025 the correct order (SPQR) was not among the options ⟦2025 Q72⟧ ⟨CHECK⟩. If this happens, choose the option that agrees with your first two parts, or skip the question. A wrong answer costs 0.25.

##Sentences of a paragraph
- **Find the opening sentence.** It introduces the topic and makes sense alone. It **never** starts with However, This, These, Such, It, Also, So, Consequently. "Recently, the company announced a new policy." ⟦2025 Q85⟧ "Blockchain technology operates on …" ⟦2025 Q90⟧
- **Follow the pointer words.** "This influx", "This lack of evidence", "This decentralization" point back to the idea just before them. ⟦2025 Q88⟧ ⟦2025 Q89⟧ ⟦2025 Q90⟧
- **Follow the turn words** (Rule 108). "However" brings a contrast, usually late in the paragraph; "So", "Consequently" and "Therefore" bring a result, usually last. ⟦2025 Q86⟧ ⟦2025 Q87⟧
- **Follow the story order**: cause before effect, problem before solution, pilot before expansion, allegation before dismissal.
- **Look for pairs.** Find two sentences that must stand together (a "this" and its idea). Most options can then be cut quickly.

#CH comprehension | Reading comprehension | SKILLS · 10 marks in 2025
Comprehension doubled from one passage to two in 2025, with five questions each ⟦2025 Q91⟧ to ⟦2025 Q100⟧. The second passage was about **Odisha's hockey** investment, so expect passages on Odisha, education, technology, society or the environment. The questions are mostly direct: the answer is written in the passage, often in nearly the same words.

##How to read a passage in five minutes
- **Read the questions first**, but not the options. Now you know what to look for.
- **Read the passage once, at normal speed.** Underline names, numbers and words like however, but and while, where the writer turns.
- **Answer from the passage, not from your own knowledge.** "Which curriculum is mentioned?" The answer is the one named: International Baccalaureate. ⟦2025 Q91⟧
- **For a "main idea" or title question**, pick the option that covers the **whole** passage, not one line. "Books as man's abiding friends" covers all of the 2021 passage. ⟦2021 Q93⟧
- **For a "challenge" or "not true" question**, look for the "however" sentence. ⟦2025 Q92⟧ ⟦2021 Q96⟧
- **For a word-meaning question**, put each option into the sentence and see which keeps the meaning. Adversity → opposite: prosperity. ⟦2021 Q94⟧
- **Watch for words copied from the passage** in wrong options. A wrong option often uses the passage's words in the wrong place.

>KEY:The 2025 Odisha passage in short
Odisha hosted the **2018 Men's Hockey World Cup** in Bhubaneswar and Rourkela (Rourkela has the world's largest hockey stadium). PSUs such as **Odisha Mining Corporation** fund athletes, called "corporate patronage". The state finds talent in **tribal regions**. Its model is "holistic": sports science, education and international coaching. Critics say hockey gets too much money compared with sports like gymnastics. ⟦2025 Q96⟧ ⟦2025 Q97⟧ ⟦2025 Q98⟧ ⟦2025 Q99⟧ ⟦2025 Q100⟧

>NEXT:Likely next
?? A passage says, "Although the scheme reached many villages, its funds were often delayed." Which is a problem with the scheme? || Delayed funds. The "although" clause gives the good side; the main clause gives the problem.
?? The best title for a passage covers what? || The whole passage, not one example or one paragraph.
?? Should you choose an option because it is true in real life? || No. Choose what the passage says.
