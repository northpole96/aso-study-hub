#CH pr-words | Probability: the words and the one formula | PROBABILITY · 2018 Q79 Q84 Q89 · 2021 Q79 Q80 Q81 Q84 Q89
Probability is the maths of chance. It gave 10 questions in 2018 and 9 in 2021, so it is the biggest block in this guide. Every one of them uses the same formula. The skill is counting correctly.

##The words
- An **experiment** is something we do whose result is not fixed: toss a coin, throw a die, pick a marble from a bag.
- An **outcome** is one possible result: heads, or the number 4, or the red marble.
- The **sample space** is the list of **all** possible outcomes. Tossing one coin: {H, T}. Throwing one die: {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}.
- An **event** is the result we are interested in: "an even number", "a prime", "a vowel".
- **Favourable outcomes** are the outcomes that make the event happen.
- A **die** is the cube with 1 to 6 dots. More than one are **dice**.
- All outcomes are taken as **equally likely** (the coin is fair, the die is fair, the pick is at random).

>KEY:The formula
**P(event) = number of favourable outcomes ÷ total number of outcomes.** Count both with care, then write the fraction.

##What the answer can be
A probability is always between **0 and 1**. 0 means it cannot happen (a 7 on one die). 1 means it must happen (a number from 1 to 6 on one die). You can write it as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage. If you get 1.2 or −0.3, you have made a mistake.

##The event and its opposite ⟦2018 Q79⟧
If p is the probability that the event **happens** and q the probability that it **does not**, then **p + q = 1**. This is the answer to 2018 Q79 (option B). It gives the commonest shortcut in probability: **P(not E) = 1 − P(E).**

⟦2021 Q79⟧ A batsman hits a boundary 15 times in 60 balls. P(boundary) = 15 ÷ 60 = 0.25. P(no boundary) = 1 − 0.25 = **0.75**.

##Picking from a list
- ⟦2018 Q84⟧ An even number from 1 to 100: 50 even numbers out of 100, **1/2**. (Options were lost in the OCR. ⟨CHECK⟩)
- ⟦2021 Q81⟧ A prime from 1 to 25. Write the primes: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23. There are 9, so **9/25**. (Do not count 1; it is not prime.)
- ⟦2021 Q84⟧ A bag has 3 green, 4 blue, 2 orange marbles (9 in all). Not orange: 3 + 4 = 7, so **7/9**.
- ⟦2018 Q85⟧ An urn has 10 white, 15 red, 25 black (50 in all). White or red: 25 ÷ 50 = **1/2**.

##Picking a letter from a word ⟦2018 Q89⟧ ⟦2021 Q89⟧
Write the letters down one by one and count them **with repeats**.

- PROBABILITY: P, R, O, B, A, B, I, L, I, T, Y is 11 letters. Vowels (A, E, I, O, U): O, A, I, I = 4. Probability **4/11**.
- ASPIRATION: A, S, P, I, R, A, T, I, O, N is 10 letters. Vowels: A, I, A, I, O = 5. Probability 5/10 = **1/2**.

The trap is to count the repeated letters once. Count each letter place as one outcome.

##The calendar question ⟦2021 Q80⟧
A year has 365 days = 52 weeks (364 days) + **1 extra day**. Each of the 52 weeks has one Sunday, so the question is whether the one extra day is a Sunday. It could be any of the 7 days, so the probability of 53 Sundays is **1/7**. In a **leap year** there are **2** extra days, so 2 of the 7 possible pairs contain a Sunday: **2/7**.

##Two numbers picked together ⟦2021 Q85⟧
x is chosen from 1, 2, 3 and y from 1, 4, 9. Find P(xy < 9). List every pair, 3 × 3 = 9 pairs: (1,1)=1, (1,4)=4, (1,9)=9, (2,1)=2, (2,4)=8, (2,9)=18, (3,1)=3, (3,4)=12, (3,9)=27. Products below 9: 1, 4, 2, 8, 3, so 5 pairs. Answer **5/9**.

>WARN:Traps in the counting
- Count **1** as not prime and **0** as even.
- "More than 3" on a die is 4, 5, 6 (not 3). "At least 3" includes 3.
- If the question says "numbers from 1 to 25", count both ends.

#CH pr-coin | Coins and dice: count with a table | PROBABILITY · 2018 Q80 Q81 Q92 · 2021 Q90 Q92
Coins and dice came in both papers, so we make a little table once and read every answer from it.

##Coins
One coin has 2 outcomes. Two coins have 2 × 2 = 4. Three coins have 2 × 2 × 2 = 8. **n coins have 2ⁿ outcomes.**

The number of ways to get a number of heads follows a pattern. For 4 coins (16 outcomes in all):

|Heads|0|1|2|3|4|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Ways|1|4|6|4|1|

So 4 coins, exactly 2 heads: **6/16 = 3/8** ⟦2021 Q90⟧. The ways come from "4 choose 2" = 6 (explained in chapter 8). For 3 coins the row is 1, 3, 3, 1.

- ⟦2018 Q81⟧ Three coins, all heads: 1 way of 8 = **1/8**. (The question number is garbled in the OCR. ⟨CHECK⟩)
- Three coins, at least one head: 1 − P(no head) = 1 − 1/8 = **7/8**.
- Three coins, exactly 2 heads: 3/8. At least 2 heads: (3 + 1)/8 = **1/2**.

##One die
Six equally likely outcomes. ⟦2021 Q92⟧ More than 3 means 4, 5, 6: 3 of 6 = **1/2**. An even number: 1/2. A number divisible by 3 (3, 6): 1/3.

##Two dice: draw the table
There are 6 × 6 = 36 outcomes. The figure shows them all, each cell holding the **total** of the two dice. Read the answer by counting the cells.

@@fig dice

- ⟦2018 Q92⟧ Total 5: (1,4) (2,3) (3,2) (4,1), 4 cells of 36 = **1/9**.
- ⟦2018 Q80⟧ Total 6: (1,5) (2,4) (3,3) (4,2) (5,1), 5 cells = **5/36**.
- Total 7: 6 cells = **1/6**, the likeliest total.
- Total 9: (3,6) (4,5) (5,4) (6,3) = 4/36 = **1/9**.
- A **doublet** (same number on both): (1,1)…(6,6) = 6/36 = **1/6**.
- Total at least 10: totals 10, 11, 12 have 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 ways: **1/6**.
- Total at most 4: totals 2, 3, 4 have 1 + 2 + 3 = 6 ways: **1/6**.

The "ways for each total" pattern, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, is worth learning. It saves drawing the table.

>WARN:Traps with dice
- (2,3) and (3,2) are **different** outcomes when two dice are thrown, so a total of 5 has 4 ways, not 2.
- "Thrown together" or "thrown once" does not change the count. Still 36.
- 2018 Q80 had "None of these" as option D, and 5/36 as A. Do not pick D by habit.

#CH pr-bag | Bags, socks, balls and cards: choosing without looking | PROBABILITY · 2018 Q91 Q93 Q85 · 2021 Q84 · 2025 Q88
When several things are drawn from a bag, listing is too slow. We use **combinations**, the number of ways to **choose** a few from many when the order does not matter.

##Combinations: nCr
**nCr** means "the number of ways to choose r things from n". Read it as "n choose r".

>KEY:The formula
**nCr = n × (n − 1) × … (r terms) ÷ r!** where r! (r factorial) means r × (r − 1) × … × 1. So 5C2 = (5 × 4) ÷ (2 × 1) = 10, and 9C2 = (9 × 8) ÷ 2 = 36.

- **nCr = nC(n − r).** 15C11 is the same as 15C4 (choose the 4 to leave out) = (15 × 14 × 13 × 12) ÷ 24 = **1,365** ⟦2025 Q88⟧.
- nC0 = nC(n) = 1. nC1 = n.
- 14C5 = (14 × 13 × 12 × 11 × 10) ÷ 120 = 2,002.

##Bag type 1: two items, same colour ⟦2018 Q91⟧
A bag has 5 brown and 4 white socks. Two are pulled out. Find P(same colour).

- Step 1: total ways to pick 2 of the 9 socks: 9C2 = 36.
- Step 2: both brown: 5C2 = 10. Both white: 4C2 = 6. Same colour: 10 + 6 = 16.
- Step 3: P = 16 ÷ 36 = **4/9**.

The word "or" (both brown **or** both white) means we **add** the two counts.

##Bag type 2: some of each colour ⟦2018 Q93⟧
A bag has 8 red and 6 blue balls. Five are drawn. Find P(3 red and 2 blue).

- Total ways: 14C5 = 2,002.
- Favourable: choose 3 of the 8 reds (8C3 = 56) **and** 2 of the 6 blues (6C2 = 15). The word "and" means we **multiply**: 56 × 15 = 840.
- P = 840 ÷ 2,002 = **60/143** (divide top and bottom by 14).

>KEY:Add for "or", multiply for "and"
Different **cases** (both brown or both white) are **added**. Different **choices that happen together** (3 reds and 2 blues) are **multiplied**.

##Bag type 3: two draws one after another
**Without replacement** means the first item is not put back, so the bag has one less. A bag has 5 red and 3 blue marbles. Two are drawn. P(both red)?

- By choosing: 5C2 ÷ 8C2 = 10 ÷ 28 = **5/14**.
- By steps: P(first red) = 5/8; then 4 reds are left among 7: 4/7. Multiply: 5/8 × 4/7 = 20/56 = **5/14**. Same.

**With replacement** the item goes back, so the bag is the same for the second draw: P(both red) = 5/8 × 5/8 = 25/64.

##Cards
A pack has **52 cards**: 4 suits (spades and clubs are black, hearts and diamonds are red), 13 cards in each suit (A, 2 to 10, J, Q, K). The **face cards** are J, Q, K: 12 in all. There are 4 aces, 4 kings and 26 red cards.

- One card drawn. P(a heart) = 13/52 = **1/4**. P(a king) = 4/52 = 1/13. P(a face card) = 12/52 = 3/13. P(a red card) = **1/2**.
- P(a king **or** a heart) = (13 + 4 − 1) ÷ 52 = 16/52 = **4/13**. We take away 1 because the king of hearts was counted twice.

#CH pr-two | Two events: AND, OR and independent events | PROBABILITY · 2018 Q90 · 2021 Q91
Some questions give the probability of **two events** A and B and ask for the chance of both, or either.

##The words
- **A ∪ B** (read "A or B") is the event that **at least one** of them happens.
- **A ∩ B** (read "A and B") is the event that **both** happen.
- **B′** (read "B not") means B does not happen. P(B′) = 1 − P(B).
- Two events are **mutually exclusive** if they cannot happen together (one die: "a 2" and "a 5"). Then P(A and B) = 0.
- Two events are **independent** if one does not change the chance of the other (two separate coin tosses).

##The two rules

>KEY:Addition and multiplication
**P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B).** We take off the "both" part because adding counted it twice. If A and B are mutually exclusive, the last part is 0.
**If A and B are independent: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).**

##Worked: find P(B) ⟦2018 Q90⟧
A and B are independent. P(A) = 0.3 and P(A or B) = 0.8. Find P(B).

- Let P(B) = p. Then P(A and B) = 0.3 × p.
- P(A or B) = 0.3 + p − 0.3p = 0.3 + 0.7p.
- Set it equal to 0.8: 0.7p = 0.5, so p = 5/7. Answer **5/7**.

##The twisted twin ⟦2021 Q91⟧
The next paper asked almost the same, but with **A or B′**: P(A) = 0.3, P(A ∪ B′) = 0.8, A and B independent. Then A and B′ are also independent. Let P(B′) = q.

- P(A or B′) = 0.3 + q − 0.3q = 0.3 + 0.7q = 0.8, so q = 5/7.
- P(B) = 1 − q = 1 − 5/7 = **2/7**.

So the twist is just one extra step at the end: from P(B′) to P(B). Read whether the question uses B or B′.

##More checks to be able to do
Let P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.5, independent.

- P(A and B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = **0.2**.
- P(A or B) = 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.2 = **0.7**.
- P(neither) = 1 − 0.7 = **0.3**. (Or 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3.)
- P(exactly one) = 0.7 − 0.2 = **0.5**.

If instead they are mutually exclusive: P(A or B) = 0.4 + 0.5 = 0.9 and P(A and B) = 0.

>WARN:Do not mix them up
**Independent** and **mutually exclusive** are different. Independent events can both happen (P(A and B) is not 0). Mutually exclusive events can never both happen. If two events with non-zero probabilities are mutually exclusive, they are **not** independent.
