#CH nationalism | The rise of nationalism (1858–1914) | PART A · STATIC
##Why it rose
English education gave Indians modern ideas of liberty. The railways, telegraph and press joined the country. British economic policy and racial insult (the Ilbert Bill, the Vernacular Press Act, the lower age limit for the ICS exam from 21 to 19 in 1876) angered the educated middle class. Lytton's rule, the Delhi Durbar (1877) in the middle of a famine and the Arms Act (1878) added fuel.

##The Congress is founded (1885)
- **A. O. Hume**, a retired British civil servant, helped to form the **Indian National Congress**. The first session was held at **Bombay (Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College), 28 to 31 December 1885**, with **W. C. Bonnerjee** as president and 72 delegates.
- The **"safety valve" theory** says Hume wanted a safe outlet for anger, so that no revolt like 1857 would come. Nationalists (Lala Lajpat Rai) said the leaders used Hume as a "lightning conductor", and used the Congress for their own aims.
- Aims of the early leaders (**Moderates**): loyalty to the Crown, a larger share of Indians in the councils and the services, simultaneous ICS exams in India, a cut in military spending. Method: **prayers, petitions and protests**.
- Moderates: Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, W. C. Bonnerjee, Surendranath Banerjee, G. K. Gokhale, Badruddin Tyabji, Ananda Mohan Bose.

##The Extremists (1905–1914)
- **Aggressive nationalists:** **Lal–Bal–Pal** = **Lala Lajpat Rai** (Punjab), **Bal Gangadhar Tilak** (Maharashtra), **Bipin Chandra Pal** (Bengal), with **Aurobindo Ghosh**.
- **Tilak:** "Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it". Newspapers **Kesari** and **Maratha**. **Ganapati** (1893) and **Shivaji festivals** (1895) for mass contact. Jailed for sedition in 1897 and 1908 (Mandalay, six years).
- Methods: **swadeshi, boycott, national education, passive resistance**.

##Partition of Bengal and the Swadeshi movement
- **Curzon** announced the partition on **16 October 1905**. Officially it was for good government. The real purpose was to weaken Bengali nationalism by splitting Hindus (West) from Muslims (East).
- Protest: **16 October** was kept as a day of mourning, with **Rakhi Bandhan**. Tagore's "**Amar Sonar Bangla**" became a song of the movement.
- **National Council of Education** (1906). **Bengal National College** (Aurobindo as principal).
- The **first national flag** was raised in Calcutta (Parsi Bagan Square) on **7 August 1906** ⟦2018 Q89⟧. **Madame Bhikaji Cama** unfurled a flag at Stuttgart in **1907**.
- The **Partition was cancelled in 1911**, when the capital shifted to Delhi. **Bengal's Odia-speaking areas and Bihar** were separated.

##The Surat split (1907)
Moderates wanted to stay with Swadeshi and boycott in Bengal only. Extremists wanted them all over India. The **session at Surat in 1907** (president Rash Behari Ghosh) broke up in a fight. Moderates stayed in the Congress and Extremists were pushed out. **Tilak** was sent to Mandalay in 1908. The two sides re-joined at **Lucknow in 1916**.

##The Muslim League
- **Simla Deputation (1906):** a group of Muslim leaders led by the **Aga Khan** met Viceroy **Minto** and asked for separate electorates.
- The **All-India Muslim League** was formed at **Dacca (30 December 1906)** under **Nawab Salimullah**. President: Aga Khan.
- **Morley–Minto Reforms (1909)** gave Muslims separate electorates.
- **Lucknow Pact (1916):** Congress and League agreed on joint demands. The Congress accepted separate electorates. Tilak and Jinnah were the main makers.

##First World War and Home Rule
- India helped Britain in the First World War (1914–18). The **Ghadar** conspirators tried an uprising.
- **Home Rule Leagues (1916)**: **Tilak** (Poona, April 1916) and **Annie Besant** (Adyar, September 1916). Demanded self-government.
- **Montagu's Declaration (20 August 1917):** "gradual development of self-governing institutions" and "responsible government". Reforms of 1919 followed.

#CH gandhi | The Gandhian age, 1915–1934 | PART A · STATIC
##Before India
**Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi** (born Porbandar, 2 October 1869). He went to South Africa in 1893 and led **satyagraha** against the pass laws and the poll tax (Natal Indian Congress 1894, Tolstoy Farm 1910, **Phoenix Settlement** 1904). He returned to India on **9 January 1915** (now Pravasi Bharatiya Divas). His political teacher was **Gokhale**. He spent a year touring India. His "four fights" were: **Champaran (1917)**, **Ahmedabad mill strike (1918)**, **Kheda (1918)** and the **Rowlatt Satyagraha (1919)**.

##Early movements
|Movement|Year|Note|
|---|---|---|
|**Champaran**|**1917**|Bihar. Against the tinkathia system of indigo. First satyagraha in India. **Rajendra Prasad, J. B. Kripalani** helped. Here he was called "**Mahatma**" (by Tagore) and "Bapu"|
|**Ahmedabad Mill Strike**|1918|Between mill owners (Ambalal Sarabhai) and workers. First hunger strike. Led to the **Ahmedabad Textile Labour Association** (1918, Gandhi) ⟦2018 Q67⟧|
|**Kheda**|1918|Gujarat. Peasants asked for relief of tax after crop failure. Vallabhbhai Patel joined|

##Rowlatt and Jallianwala
- **Rowlatt Act (1919):** the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act. It allowed arrest without trial and trial without a jury. Called the "**Black Act**".
- **Rowlatt Satyagraha:** Gandhi called a hartal on 6 April 1919. Dr **Satya Pal** and **Dr Saifuddin Kitchlew** were arrested at Amritsar.
- **Jallianwala Bagh, 13 April 1919 (Baisakhi).** Brigadier-General **Reginald Dyer** ordered his troops to fire without warning on a peaceful crowd in a closed garden at Amritsar. Hundreds were killed (the Government said 379, the Congress counted about 1,000). **Hunter Committee** (1919) to inquire. **Michael O'Dwyer**, Lieutenant-Governor of Punjab, was shot in London in 1940 by **Udham Singh**. **Rabindranath Tagore** gave up his knighthood. Gandhi returned his Kaiser-i-Hind medal.

##Khilafat and Non-Cooperation (1920–22)
- The **Khilafat** movement (1919) protested at the British treatment of the Sultan of Turkey. Leaders: the **Ali brothers (Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali)**, Maulana Azad.
- **Non-Cooperation** was passed at the special Calcutta session (September 1920) and confirmed at **Nagpur** (December 1920). Programme: give up titles, boycott councils, courts, schools and foreign cloth; take up the **charkha** and **khadi**; set up national schools.
- **Chauri Chaura, 5 February 1922** (Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh): a mob burnt a police station and killed 22 policemen. Gandhi **suspended** the movement on 12 February (Bardoli resolution). He was arrested in March 1922 and got six years.
- **Gandhi's first visit to Odisha** was on **23 March 1921** (Cuttack) ⟦2018 Q31⟧. See Chapter 12.

##Swaraj Party and the quiet years (1923–27)
- **Swaraj Party:** formed by **C. R. Das** and **Motilal Nehru** on 1 January 1923 (Gaya Congress, December 1922). It wanted to **enter the councils** and wreck them from inside ("No-changers" were Gandhians).
- **Hindustan Republican Association** (HRA) 1924: Sachindra Nath Sanyal and Ram Prasad Bismil.
- **Kakori** (1925). Death of C. R. Das (1925). Founding of the **RSS** (1925, Nagpur, K. B. Hedgewar).
- **Labour and left:** **AITUC** (1920), **Communist Party of India** (1920 Tashkent; 1925 Kanpur), **Meerut Conspiracy Case** (1929).

##The Simon Commission and the Nehru Report
- **Simon Commission (1927):** all seven members British. India reacted with "**Simon Go Back**". On 30 October 1928 **Lala Lajpat Rai** was lathi-charged at Lahore and died on 17 November. "Every blow on my body will be a nail in the coffin of British rule."
- **Nehru Report (1928)**, a plan of a constitution by **Motilal Nehru**. Jinnah's **Fourteen Points** (1929) replied.
- **Bardoli Satyagraha (1928)** led by Patel. He got the title "**Sardar**".

##Purna Swaraj and Civil Disobedience (1929–31)
- **Irwin's Declaration** (October 1929). **Lahore Congress** (December 1929), president **Jawaharlal Nehru**: resolved on **Purna Swaraj**. **26 January 1930** was kept as **Independence Day** (Purna Swaraj Day) ⟦2025 Q54⟧.
- **Dandi March:** Gandhi and **78 followers** left **Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930** and walked about 240 miles to **Dandi**. On **6 April 1930** he **broke the salt law** ⟦2018 Q32⟧.
- **Civil Disobedience Movement:** salt laws, boycott of foreign cloth and liquor, refusal to pay tax. **Dharasana Salt Works** raid led by Sarojini Naidu and Imam Saheb. **C. Rajagopalachari** led a march at **Vedaranyam** (Tamil Nadu). **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan** in the Frontier (Khudai Khidmatgar, "**Frontier Gandhi**"). **Peshawar: Garhwal Rifles refused to fire.** In Odisha: Inchudi and Kujang (see Chapter 12). In Manipur: **Rani Gaidinliu**.
- **First Round Table Conference** (November 1930, London): Congress did not attend.
- **Gandhi–Irwin Pact (5 March 1931):** Civil Disobedience suspended; political prisoners released; salt allowed for home use; Gandhi to attend the next conference. The pact was criticised because **Bhagat Singh was hanged** (23 March 1931).
- **Karachi Congress (March 1931):** Patel presided. Resolutions on **Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme**. ⟦2025 Q31⟧
- **Second Round Table Conference:** September–December 1931, **Gandhi** attended. No result.

##Communal Award and Poona Pact (1932)
- **Communal Award (16 August 1932):** Ramsay MacDonald gave separate electorates to Muslims, Sikhs, Christians, Europeans, **and the depressed classes**.
- Gandhi began a fast unto death at Yerwada jail (20 September). **Poona Pact (24 September 1932)** with **B. R. Ambedkar**: no separate electorates, but **148 reserved seats** (instead of 71) for depressed classes in joint electorates.
- **Harijan Sevak Sangh** (1932). Gandhi's paper **Harijan** (1933).
- **Third Round Table** (1932) was the last. The Civil Disobedience movement ended in 1934.

>KEY:Order of the big four
**Rowlatt (1919) → Simon boycott (1928) → Civil Disobedience (1930) → Quit India (1942).** The 2018 paper asked for the order of four movements. ⟦2018 Q87⟧

#CH final | Provinces, war, Quit India and Independence, 1935–1950 | PART A · STATIC
##Provincial elections and ministries (1937–39)
- Under the **Act of 1935**, elections were held in **1937** in 11 provinces. The **Congress** won in 8 and formed ministries in 7 or 8 (Madras, Bihar, Orissa, UP, CP, Bombay, NWFP, Assam). The **League** did poorly.
- In Odisha **Biswanath Das** formed the first Congress ministry (1937) and **Harekrushna Mahtab** later (1946).
- The **Muslim League** said the Congress rule was "Hindu raj". It marked **22 December 1939** as a **"Day of Deliverance"** after the Congress ministries resigned (October–November 1939) over India being dragged into the war.

##Congress politics, 1938–40
- **Subhas Chandra Bose** was Congress president in 1938 (Haripura) and re-elected in 1939 at **Tripuri**, defeating Gandhi's choice, **Pattabhi Sitaramayya**. He resigned and founded the **Forward Bloc**.
- **Lahore Resolution (Pakistan Resolution), 23 March 1940:** moved by **A. K. Fazlul Huq**, in the presidency of **Jinnah**. Demanded "independent states" for Muslim-majority areas.
- **August Offer (8 August 1940):** Linlithgow. Rejected.
- **Individual Satyagraha (October 1940):** first satyagrahi **Vinoba Bhave**, second **Nehru**. Called also the "Delhi Chalo" movement.

##Cripps Mission and Quit India (1942)
- **Cripps Mission (March 1942):** offered dominion status after the war, with the right to leave. Rejected. Gandhi called it "a post-dated cheque on a crashing bank".
- **Quit India Movement:** the **Wardha** Congress Working Committee resolution (14 July); the **Bombay (Gowalia Tank) session of 8 August 1942** adopted it. Gandhi gave the call "**Do or Die**" ("Karenge ya Marenge"). All top leaders were arrested on 9 August. **Aruna Asaf Ali** hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank. **Usha Mehta** ran the **Congress Radio** (secret). **Jayaprakash Narayan** escaped from Hazaribagh jail. Parallel governments: **Ballia (Chittu Pandey)**, **Tamluk (Midnapore, Jatiya Sarkar, Matangini Hazra)**, **Satara (Prati Sarkar, Y. B. Chavan, Nana Patil)**. In Odisha: **Eram** (Balasore), Nilagiri, Koraput.
- **Jinnah** and the League, the Communists, Hindu Mahasabha and princes kept away or opposed it.
- The British crushed it with severe force. About **940 were killed** (official figure) in firing.

##Subhas Bose and the INA
- Bose escaped from house arrest in Calcutta in **January 1941**, via Afghanistan, to Germany. He raised the **Indian Legion** in Germany. In **1943** he travelled by submarine to Japanese-held Asia.
- The first **Indian National Army** was formed by **Mohan Singh** and **Rash Behari Bose** (**Indian Independence League**) with prisoners of war in 1942. Bose took command on **4 July 1943** in Singapore.
- **Provisional Government of Free India (Azad Hind Government): 21 October 1943, Singapore** ⟦2018 Q33⟧. Capital later at Rangoon. Japan handed the **Andaman and Nicobar islands** to it.
- Brigades: **Gandhi, Azad, Nehru** and the women's **Rani Jhansi Regiment** (Captain **Lakshmi Sahgal**). Slogans: "**Dilli Chalo**", "**Jai Hind**", "Give me blood and I will give you freedom".
- The INA reached **Imphal and Kohima** (1944) but failed. The **INA trials at the Red Fort** (1945–46): Shah Nawaz Khan, P. K. Sahgal and Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon, defended by Nehru and Bhulabhai Desai. The trials caused huge protest.
- Bose is believed to have died in an air crash at Taihoku (Taiwan) on **18 August 1945** ⟨CHECK; disputed⟩.

##Partition and princely states
- The **Radcliffe Line** (Sir Cyril Radcliffe) fixed the new borders in Punjab and Bengal. It was announced on 17 August 1947. About 14 to 15 million people moved. Hundreds of thousands were killed.
- There were **565 princely states**. The **Indian Independence Act** said that British paramountcy lapsed. **Sardar Patel** and **V. P. Menon** persuaded almost all to join by the **Instrument of Accession**. **Junagadh** (Muslim Nawab, Hindu majority): joined Pakistan; India took it after a plebiscite (February 1948). **Hyderabad**: **Operation Polo (September 1948)**. **Kashmir**: Maharaja Hari Singh signed on **26 October 1947** after Pakistani tribal raids.
- **Gandhi** was killed by **Nathuram Godse** on **30 January 1948** at Birla House, Delhi.
- The Constitution came into force on **26 January 1950**.

>WARN:Mahatma Gandhi and Independence Day
Gandhi was in **Calcutta** (Noakhali, Hydari Manzil) working for peace and did **not** attend the 15 August 1947 function in Delhi.

##Leaders in one line
- **Dadabhai Naoroji:** "Grand Old Man of India". Drain theory. First Indian in the British Parliament (1892).
- **Tilak:** "Lokmanya". Father of Indian unrest.
- **Gokhale:** Gandhi's political guru. Servants of India Society.
- **Lala Lajpat Rai:** "Punjab Kesari". First AITUC president.
- **Surendranath Banerjee:** "Surrender Not"/"Rashtraguru" ("uncrowned king" of Bengal).
- **Patel:** "Iron Man". Bardoli. United the princely states.
- **Bose:** "Netaji".
- **Gopabandhu Das:** "Utkalmani".
- **Madan Mohan Malaviya:** BHU. President of the Congress four times.
- **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan:** "Frontier Gandhi".
- **C. Rajagopalachari:** "Rajaji". Last Governor-General.
- **Maulana Azad:** Youngest Congress president (1923); first education minister.
- **Annie Besant:** Home Rule. First woman Congress president.
