##Company rule (1773–1857)
|Act|What was new|
|---|---|
|Regulating Act, 1773|First step to control the Company. **Governor-General of Bengal** (Warren Hastings). Governor-General's council of four. **Supreme Court at Calcutta** (1774)|
|Pitt's India Act, 1784|**Board of Control** in London. The Company's work was split into commercial (Directors) and political (Board). "Double government"|
|Charter Act, 1793|Renewed the Company for 20 years|

|Charter Act, 1833|**Governor-General of India** (Bentinck). A **Law Member** (Macaulay). Company's trade ended completely. Said no Indian could be barred from a job because of religion or birth|
|Charter Act, 1853|Separate Legislative Council. **Open competition** for the civil service. Last Charter Act|

##Crown rule (1858–1947)
|Act|What was new|
|---|---|
|Government of India Act, 1858|Rule passed from the Company to the **Crown**. Post of **Secretary of State for India** with a council of 15. **Viceroy**|
|Indian Councils Act, 1861|Indians **nominated** to the Viceroy's council (three were: Raja of Benares, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao). Portfolio system. Legislative councils for Bombay and Madras|
|Indian Councils Act, 1892|First step to **election** (indirect). Members could discuss the budget and ask questions ⟦2018 Q28⟧|
|Indian Councils Act, 1909 (**Morley–Minto**)|**Separate electorates for Muslims**. Elected members increased. An Indian (S. P. Sinha) in the Viceroy's council. Minto is called the father of communal electorates|
|Government of India Act, 1919 (**Montagu–Chelmsford**)|**Dyarchy in provinces**: some subjects "transferred" to Indian ministers and some "reserved" for the Governor. **Two Houses** at the Centre (Council of State, Legislative Assembly). Direct elections. A statutory commission after 10 years. Franchise only to about 10% people|
|Government of India Act, 1935|**Provincial autonomy.** **All-India Federation** (never came about). **Federal Court** (1937). **Reserve Bank** (1935). Federal Public Service Commission. Separate electorates extended. Burma separated. **Odisha and Sind made provinces (1936)** ⟦2025 Q38⟧. Council of India abolished. It became the base of the Indian Constitution|
|Indian Independence Act, 1947|**Two dominions**, India and Pakistan, from 15 August 1947. Princely states free to join either. The post of Secretary of State ended|

#CH economy | Land, trade and money: the economic story | PART A · STATIC
##Land revenue systems
The British needed tax from land. They tried three ways.
|System|Who started|Where|Who paid the tax|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Permanent Settlement** (Zamindari)|Cornwallis, **1793**|Bengal, Bihar, Odisha, Varanasi|The **zamindar**. Amount fixed for ever. Zamindars could lose the land if they did not pay on the "sunset" day|
|**Ryotwari**|Thomas Munro (Madras, **1820**); Bombay too|**Madras and Bombay** ⟦2025 Q88⟧|The farmer (**ryot**) directly. Revised every 20 to 30 years|
|**Mahalwari**|Holt Mackenzie, **1822** (Regulation VII)|North-Western Provinces, Punjab, Central India|The whole **village** (mahal) together|

In all three the peasant paid heavily. About half the produce went as tax. Under the Permanent Settlement, zamindars grew rich while tenants were crushed.

##Drain of wealth and de-industrialisation
- **Drain of wealth:** India sent a large part of its income to Britain without return: salaries, pensions, interest on debt and profits. **Dadabhai Naoroji** gave this theory in his book **Poverty and Un-British Rule in India** (1901). **R. C. Dutt** wrote **The Economic History of India** (1901). M. G. Ranade and G. Subramania Iyer also wrote on it.
- **De-industrialisation:** Cheap British cloth from the factories of Lancashire was allowed into India nearly free, while Indian cloth was taxed in Britain. Weavers and craftsmen lost work and went back to farming. Nationalist writers hold that colonial policy caused it, and that is the standard answer. ⟦2021 Q27⟧
- **Commercialisation of agriculture:** farmers were forced to grow cash crops such as **indigo**, cotton, jute, opium and tea for the British market.

##Famines
|Famine|Year|Note|
|---|---|---|
|Bengal|1770|Killed about a third of the people|
|**Odisha (Na'anka)**|1866|Killed about a third of Odisha's people. Led to a Famine Commission (Campbell)|
|South India|1876–78|Under Lytton. Strachey Commission (1880)|
|All India|1896–97, 1899–1900|Lyall Commission (1898), MacDonnell Commission (1901)|
|Bengal|1943|About three million died during the war|

After 1880 the Government made a "Famine Code" and later the Irrigation Commission (1901).

##Railways, telegraph, irrigation
- **First railway:** Bombay to Thane, **16 April 1853** (Dalhousie). First line in the north: Howrah to Hooghly (1854). Rail laid so that raw cotton could go to ports and British goods to the interior.
- **Telegraph:** 1851 (Calcutta to Diamond Harbour) and a wide network by 1854. **Post Office Act**, 1854.
- **Irrigation:** Ganga Canal (1854), Punjab canal colonies.
- **Money:** the **Reserve Bank of India** was set up on **1 April 1935**, from the Hilton Young Commission (1926) idea and under the Act of 1934.

##Industry and early Indian business
- **Tata Iron and Steel Company** at Jamshedpur, 1907 (Jamsetji Tata). First Indian cotton mill: Bombay 1854 (Cowasji Davar). First jute mill at Rishra, near Calcutta, in 1855 (British owned).
- The **Swadeshi movement** (1905) gave Indian industries a push: Bengal Chemicals (1893 by P. C. Ray), Tata, Bank of India.
- **Discriminating protection** policy came in the 1920s. The Fiscal Commission of 1921–22.
- **The Bombay Plan (1944)** by industrialists, a plan for post-war growth.

##The plantation and the indigo story
European planters forced peasants in Bengal and Bihar to grow indigo at fixed low prices. The **Indigo Revolt** of 1859–60 in Bengal, led by Digambar Biswas and Bishnucharan Biswas, was helped by Harish Chandra Mukherjee's **Hindoo Patriot** and by **Dinabandhu Mitra's play Nil Darpan** (1860). The Government set up the Indigo Commission. In Bihar the **Champaran Satyagraha (1917)** was against the **tinkathia** system of 3 out of 20 parts to be sown with indigo. It was Gandhi's first big movement in India.

##The Revolt of 1857
**Spark.** The new **Enfield rifle** had cartridges that were said to be greased with cow and pig fat. Both Hindu and Muslim soldiers feared for their religion.

**Deeper causes.**
- **Political:** states taken by the Doctrine of Lapse, **Awadh** annexed in 1856, pensions ended (Nana Saheb), the Mughal emperor's title to end after Bahadur Shah.
- **Economic:** heavy land tax, ruin of crafts, loss of work.
- **Social:** fear of conversion, laws against sati and for widow remarriage seen as meddling.
- **Military:** Indian soldiers were low paid and badly treated. The **General Service Enlistment Act (1856)** required service overseas.

**Course.**
- **29 March 1857:** **Mangal Pandey** attacked officers at **Barrackpore**.
- **10 May 1857:** the revolt proper began at **Meerut**. ⟦2018 Q29⟧ Next day soldiers reached **Delhi** and proclaimed **Bahadur Shah Zafar** emperor.

**Suppression.**
- **British officers:** John Nicholson and **Hodson** (took Delhi, September 1857; Hodson captured the emperor at Humayun's Tomb), **Henry Havelock** (Kanpur), **Colin Campbell** (Lucknow), **Hugh Rose** (Jhansi, Gwalior).
- **Bahadur Shah** was exiled to Rangoon where he died in 1862. Tantia Tope was betrayed and hanged (April 1859). The Rani of Jhansi died fighting at Gwalior (June 1858). Kunwar Singh died in April 1858.
- Sikh and Gurkha soldiers, the Madras and Bombay armies and many princes (Scindia, Holkar, the Nizam, Kashmir) **stayed loyal**.

**Result.**
- The **Government of India Act, 1858** ended Company rule. The Crown took over.
- **Queen Victoria's Proclamation** was read at **Allahabad on 1 November 1858**. It promised no more annexation, respect for religion and equal jobs. It is called the **"Magna Carta of Indian liberties"**.
- The Army was reorganised (Peel Commission, 1858) and "divide and rule" grew.

**How it is judged.**
- **V. D. Savarkar** called it **the "First War of Independence"** (book, 1909).
- **R. C. Majumdar** said it was "neither first, nor national, nor a war of independence".
- **S. N. Sen** and **Disraeli** (a "national revolt") took middle views.
