Article 01 · How the Constitution was made

Two years, eleven months, eighteen days

A body called the Constituent Assembly wrote the Constitution. Learn five dates and four names and this topic is done.

3 minAsked: 2025

The Constituent Assembly was set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan of 1946. It met for the first time on 9 December 1946. Sachchidananda Sinha chaired that first meeting as the temporary chairman. Dr Rajendra Prasad was then elected the permanent President of the Assembly.

Four days later, on 13 December 1946, Jawaharlal Nehru moved the Objectives Resolution. Its ideas later became the Preamble.

The Drafting Committee was formed on 29 August 1947. Dr B.R. Ambedkar was its chairman, and B.N. Rau was the constitutional adviser.

Adopted in November, alive in January

The Assembly adopted the Constitution on 26 November 1949. This is now Constitution Day. It came into force on 26 January 1950.

Why 26 January? Because on 26 January 1930 the Congress had celebrated "Purna Swaraj Day", the day it declared complete independence as its goal 2025 Q54.

Memory trickNovember is for Name on paper (adopted). January is for Joining life (in force). 26 Nov = Constitution Day, 26 Jan = Republic Day.

The whole job took 2 years, 11 months and 18 days.

At a glance

1946Cabinet Mission Plan set up the Assembly
9 Decfirst meeting, 1946
Prasadpermanent President of the Assembly
Rauconstitutional adviser

Asked before

  • 2025 Q54Why 26 January? → Purna Swaraj Day, 1930

1-minute recapHow it was made

  • Set up under the Cabinet Mission Plan, 1946. First met 9 Dec 1946 (Sinha temporary; Rajendra Prasad President).
  • Objectives Resolution: Nehru, 13 Dec 1946. It became the Preamble.
  • Drafting Committee: 29 Aug 1947, chairman Ambedkar, adviser B.N. Rau.
  • Adopted 26 Nov 1949; in force 26 Jan 1950 (Purna Swaraj Day link).

Article 02 · The Preamble

The opening page, and India's identity card

The Preamble is the opening paragraph. It says what kind of country India is and what it promises to its people.

4 minAsked: 2018, 2025

India is a Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic Republic. The Preamble promises its people Justice (social, economic and political), Liberty, Equality and Fraternity.

Changed only once

The words Socialist, Secular and Integrity were added by the 42nd Amendment in 1976 2025 Q32. That is the only time the Preamble has been changed.

Is it part of the Constitution?

The Supreme Court changed its mind. In the Berubari case (1960) it said no. In the Kesavananda Bharati case (1973) it said yes. So today three things are true:

  • The Preamble is part of the Constitution.
  • It can be amended, as long as the basic structure is not damaged.
  • You cannot go to court to enforce it.
TrapThe 2018 paper asked what is NOT correct about the Preamble 2018 Q34 and gave two false options: "it cannot be amended" and "it is enforceable in a court". Both are false. It can be amended; it cannot be enforced.

Twelve drawers at the back of the book

Schedules are lists kept at the end of the Constitution. The usual question is "which pair is wrong?"

Think of the Schedules as a cabinet with twelve labelled drawers. The exam gives you four drawer labels and asks which one is on the wrong drawer 2025 Q77. In 2025 the wrong pair was "Second Schedule: oaths". Oaths are in the Third.

Article 04 · Fundamental Rights

Six pillars you can take to court

Fundamental Rights are in Part III, Articles 12 to 35. Unlike the Directive Principles, you can go to court to enforce them.

5 minAsked: 2018, 2021, 2025

There are six groups of rights. The Article ranges are asked directly 2025 Q82, so learn the numbers as well as the names.

RightArticlesMain points
Equality14–18Equal before law (14), no discrimination (15), equal chance in government jobs (16), untouchability ended (17), titles ended (18)
Freedom19–22Six freedoms (19), protection when accused (20), life and liberty (21), education for ages 6–14 (21A), protection on arrest (22)
Against exploitation23–24No forced labour or trafficking (23), no child labour in factories and mines (24)
Religion25–28Follow and spread religion, manage religious affairs, no tax for a religion, no religious teaching in government schools
Culture and education29–30Minorities can protect their language and run their own schools
Remedies32Go straight to the Supreme Court if a right is broken

Property is no longer a Fundamental Right

The 44th Amendment (1978) removed Article 19(1)(f) and Article 31 2021 Q39. Property is now only a legal right under Article 300A.

Who can limit the freedoms?

Only the legislature, by making a law. In the exam the answer is Parliament 2021 Q32.

Article 21 keeps growing

Courts have read many rights into "life and liberty". In the Puttaswamy case (2017), nine judges led by Chief Justice J.S. Khehar agreed that privacy is a Fundamental Right. It is not absolute; a fair law can limit it 2018 Q16. In 2025 the Supreme Court said maternity leave is part of a woman's reproductive rights under Article 21, even for a third child 2025 Q6.

Article 21A (education, ages 6 to 14) came with the 86th Amendment in 2002.

Is voting a Fundamental Right?

No. The right to vote comes from Article 326 and election law 2021 Q34. It is definitely not Fundamental or Natural. Courts have called it a legal right and, more recently, a constitutional right. Answer keys differ, so don't lose sleep over this one.

At a glance

12–35Articles in Part III
6groups of rights
300Aproperty, now a legal right
2017privacy (Puttaswamy, 9 judges)

Asked before

  • 2025 Q82Right to religion → Articles 25 to 28

1-minute recapFundamental Rights

  • Equality 14–18 · Freedom 19–22 · Exploitation 23–24 · Religion 25–28 · Culture 29–30 · Remedies 32.
  • Property removed by 44th (1978); now Art. 300A.
  • Only Parliament (by law) can put reasonable restrictions.
  • Privacy: Puttaswamy 2017. 21A education: 86th, 2002. Voting: Art. 326, not a Fundamental Right.

Article 05 · Writs

Five court orders with Latin names

A writ is a court order. The Supreme Court issues writs under Article 32 and the High Courts under Article 226.

3 minAsked: 2018

Each writ is named after its first Latin words. Learn the meaning of the name and you will know what the writ does.

WritMeaningUse
Habeas corpus"Produce the body"Bring a detained person before the court
Mandamus"We command"Orders a public officer to do his duty 2018 Q38
Prohibition"Stop"A higher court stops a lower court while the case is going on
Certiorari"Send us the file"A higher court cancels an order already passed
Quo warranto"By what authority?"Questions a person's right to hold a public post
TrapProhibition and Certiorari both go from a higher court to a lower one. The difference is timing. Prohibition comes before the order (stop the case). Certiorari comes after the order (cancel it).

Article 35A and Jammu and Kashmir

Article 35A protected the special rights of permanent residents of Jammu and Kashmir 2018 Q35. It ended when Article 370 was removed on 5 August 2019. Jammu and Kashmir, and Ladakh, became Union Territories on 31 October 2019.

1-minute recapWrits

  • SC: Art. 32 · HC: Art. 226.
  • Habeas corpus = produce the body · Mandamus = we command · Quo warranto = by what authority.
  • Prohibition = stop before the order · Certiorari = cancel after the order.
  • 35A ended with Art. 370 (5 Aug 2019); J&K and Ladakh became UTs on 31 Oct 2019.

Article 06 · Directive Principles and Duties

Goals for the government, duties for us

Directive Principles (Articles 36 to 51) tell the government where to go. Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) tell citizens what to do.

4 minAsked: 2025

Directive Principles are goals for the government. You cannot go to court to enforce them. The idea came from Ireland. These are the ones to know:

ArticleGoal
39AEqual justice and free legal aid
40Set up village panchayats
44Uniform Civil Code
45Early childhood care
48Organise farming; stop cow slaughter
48AProtect environment, forests and wildlife
50Separate the judiciary from the executive
51Promote international peace (the foreign policy Article) 2025 Q78

Four added in 1976

The 42nd Amendment added free legal aid (39A), healthy growth of children (39(f)), workers' share in management (43A) and environment (48A) 2025 Q98.

Fundamental Duties

Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) were also added in 1976, on the advice of the Swaran Singh Committee. The idea came from the USSR. There were 10. The 86th Amendment (2002) added the 11th: parents must send children aged 6 to 14 to school.

Memory trickThe 86th Amendment gave education twice: a right for the child (21A) and a duty for the parent (11th duty).

  • 42nd added 39A, 39(f), 43A, 48A.
  • Duties: 51A, 1976, Swaran Singh, from USSR; 11th duty by 86th (2002).

Article 08 · President, Vice-President, Prime Minister

Who elects whom at the top

The exam loves one question here: who votes for the President, and who votes for the Vice-President. The drawing answers it.

5 minAsked: 2018, 2021, 2025

President (Articles 52 to 62)

  • Minimum age 35. Term 5 years.
  • Elected by an electoral college: elected MPs of both Houses + elected MLAs of all states, Delhi and Puducherry (Article 54).
  • Method: proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot. This is in Article 55(3) 2021 Q36.
  • Removed by impeachment (Article 61) for "violation of the Constitution".
  • Can pardon (Article 72) and issue ordinances when Parliament is not sitting (Article 123).
  • Addresses Parliament at the first session after each general election and at the first session of every year (Article 87) 2018 Q66.

Vice-President (Articles 63 to 71)

  • Elected by all members of both Houses, elected and nominated. MLAs do not vote 2018 Q36.
  • He is the Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
  • The President is Droupadi Murmu. The Vice-President is C.P. Radhakrishnan, elected in September 2025 after Jagdeep Dhankhar resigned check before exam
Memory trickPresident: MLAs in, nominated out. Vice-President: nominated in, MLAs out.

Prime Minister and Council of Ministers (Articles 74 and 75)

  • The President appoints the PM. Other ministers are appointed on the PM's advice, and the PM gives them their departments.
  • The PM can be from either House. Indira Gandhi and Manmohan Singh were Rajya Sabha members 2025 Q18.
  • The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha.
  • A non-MP can become minister or PM, but must become an MP within six months.
  • The 91st Amendment (2003): the Council of Ministers cannot be bigger than 15% of the Lok Sabha 2025 Q91. Same 15% in states, minimum 12 ministers.
TrapThe Attorney General is under Article 76, appointed by the President. He can speak in Parliament but cannot vote. The 2018 paper printed "Article 1976" as a trap option 2018 Q90.

At a glance

35minimum age, President
55(3)single transferable vote
15%ministers cap (91st)
6months for a non-MP minister

Asked before

  • 2025 Q18"PM must be from Lok Sabha" → false
  • 2025 Q9191st → ministers ≤ 15% of Lok Sabha
  • 2021 Q36Single transferable vote → Art. 55(3)
  • 2018 Q36Who elects the VP → all members of both Houses
  • 2018 Q66President's address → Art. 87

1-minute recapPresident, VP, PM

  • President: 35, 5 years, elected MPs + elected MLAs (incl. Delhi, Puducherry), Art. 55(3) STV.
  • Impeachment 61 · pardon 72 · ordinance 123 · address 87.
  • VP: all MPs incl. nominated, no MLAs; Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
  • PM from either House; 15% cap (91st); AG = Art. 76, speaks but can't vote.

Article 09 · Parliament

Two Houses and three kinds of bills

The longest article in this issue, and one of the most asked. Read it in two sittings if you need to.

6 minAsked: 2018, 2021, 2025
Lok SabhaRajya Sabha
Members now543245 (233 elected + 12 nominated)
Minimum age2530
Term5 yearsPermanent House. A member sits 6 years; one-third retire every 2 years
How electedDirectly by people, First Past the Post 2025 Q83By MLAs, single transferable vote
Presiding officerSpeakerVice-President (as Chairman)

First Past the Post means the candidate with the most votes wins, even without a majority. It is used for the Lok Sabha and State Assemblies. Single transferable vote is used for the President, Vice-President, Rajya Sabha and Legislative Councils.

The first Speaker of the Lok Sabha was G.V. Mavalankar 2018 Q79. The Speaker decides whether a bill is a Money Bill, chairs a joint sitting, and decides defection cases.

Three kinds of bills

BillStarts inRajya Sabha's powerJoint sitting?
OrdinaryEither HouseEqual to Lok SabhaYes (Art. 108)
Money (Art. 110)Only Lok SabhaMust return in 14 days; can only suggest. Lok Sabha may accept or reject 2025 Q19No
Amendment (Art. 368)Either HouseEqual. Special majority in each HouseNo

There have been only three joint sittings: Dowry Prohibition (1961), Banking Service Commission (1978) and POTA (2002).

Budget words

  • The Constitution calls the budget the Annual Financial Statement (Article 112).
  • Vote on Account: money given in advance so the government can run for about two months until the full budget is passed 2018 Q37.
  • Appropriation Bill: permission to take money out of the Consolidated Fund.
  • Finance Bill: the tax proposals. Supplementary grant: extra money when the approved amount falls short.

Special powers of each House

  • Only Rajya Sabha 2025 Q59: allow Parliament to make a law on a State List subject (Art. 249); allow a new All India Service (Art. 312). Both need a two-thirds vote.
  • Only Lok Sabha: Money Bills and no-confidence motions.
  • Both together: electing the Vice-President, creating or ending Legislative Councils, changing state boundaries.
Trap"How can a new All India Service be created?" 2021 Q45 It takes two steps: a Rajya Sabha resolution, then a law by Parliament. The first step is what makes it special, so the usual answer is a resolution of the Rajya Sabha.

245Rajya Sabha (233 + 12)

1-minute recapParliament

  • LS 543, age 25, FPTP, Speaker. RS 245 (233 + 12), age 30, STV, 1/3 retire every 2 years.
  • Money Bill: LS only; RS returns in 14 days, suggestions only; no joint sitting.
  • Joint sittings: Dowry 1961, Banking 1978, POTA 2002. Speaker presides.

Article 10 · Courts, States and local government

Judges, Governors and village councils

From the Supreme Court down to the village panchayat: the numbers and dates the exam keeps asking.

5 minAsked: 2018, 2021, 2025

Courts

  • The Supreme Court has the Chief Justice + 33 judges = 34. Supreme Court judges retire at 65, High Court judges at 62.
  • Judges are chosen through the collegium (a group of senior judges).
  • The 99th Amendment tried to replace the collegium with a National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC). It came into force on 13 April 2015 2021 Q37 and was struck down in October 2015.
  • The present Chief Justice is Justice Surya Kant (53rd, since 24 November 2025). His term ends in February 2027 check before exam

The States

  • The Governor is appointed by the President and stays "during the pleasure of the President". The Sarkaria Commission said Governors should be well-known people from outside the state who are not active in politics 2025 Q89.
  • Legislative Council: only six states have one: Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka. Odisha has none.
  • To create or abolish a Council, the State Assembly passes a resolution and then Parliament makes a law (Article 169) 2021 Q35. Minimum age for a member is 30 2025 Q33.
  • Delhi became the National Capital Territory by the 69th Amendment (1991) 2018 Q75.

Local government

  • The Balwant Rai Mehta Committee (1957) suggested three-level Panchayati Raj: village, block, district. Rajasthan started first (Nagaur, 2 October 1959). This committee is about panchayats, not civil services 2025 Q41.
  • 73rd Amendment: panchayats, 11th Schedule, in force 24 April 1993 (National Panchayati Raj Day).
  • 74th Amendment: municipalities, 12th Schedule, in force 1 June 1993 2018 Q40.
  • Local body elections are run by the State Election Commission, not the Election Commission of India 2025 Q34.

6states with a Council
24 Apr73rd in force, 1993

1-minute recapCourts and States

  • SC: 34 judges, retire 65. HC: retire 62. Collegium; NJAC (99th) struck down 2015.
  • Governor: appointed by President; Sarkaria: non-partisan outsider.
  • Councils in AP, Telangana, UP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka (not Odisha); Art. 169; age 30.

Article 13 · Emergencies and confusing laws

Three doors, and one was never opened

The Constitution has three kinds of emergency. Learn the Article number on each door.

3 minAsked: 2018
EmergencyArticleKey fact
National352For war, external attack or armed rebellion. Declared three times: 1962, 1971, 1975
President's Rule356When a state government cannot run by the Constitution. Up to 3 years
Financial360Never declared

The words for a National Emergency changed in 1978. The 44th Amendment replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion". The 1975 Emergency had been declared on the ground of internal disturbance, so the change made a repeat harder.

Central law or state law?

The exam once asked which of these laws was made by a state 2018 Q77. MCOCA (Maharashtra Control of Organised Crime Act) is a state law. AFSPA, POTA and FEMA were made by Parliament.

Memory trickThe name gives it away: a law with a state's name in it (Maharashtra…) is a state law.
